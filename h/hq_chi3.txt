DE: Central Mendoza has the longest history of quality wine and many famous producers. DE: Patagonia comprises Río Negro and the relatively new Neuquén Province. With vineyards at about 200–250 m, cooling comes from latitude, not altitude. Strong desert winds are a challenge, but low rainfall and a wide diurnal range mean little disease. Long daylight hours and cool nights give wines with concentrated but fresh fruit and medium to high acidity. Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Noir, Malbec and Merlot can all make premium wines in Río Negro; Neuquén's full potential is still being explored.
