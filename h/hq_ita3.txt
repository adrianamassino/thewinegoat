DE: Northern Italian vineyards were traditionally planted with high-yielding vines at low density, often on pergolas: the canopy is trained high with fruit hanging below a horizontal roof of leaves, protecting it from sunburn and allowing airflow to minimise rot. Pergolas are still used for high-acid, low-sugar grapes (for sparkling) and for grapes to be dried, which must be healthy, but higher-density VSP plantings are now more common.
