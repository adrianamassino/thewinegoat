DE: The Port industry is split between the twin coastal cities of Porto and Vila Nova de Gaia, on opposite sides of the Douro's mouth, and the vineyard area starting 70 km upstream. There are three sub-regions: Baixo Corgo produces the lightest wines; Cima Corgo has the greatest number of top vineyards; Douro Superior, still sparsely planted, is also renowned for top-quality wine. The climate is warm continental thanks to the Serra do Marão blocking Atlantic winds, but not uniform: Baixo Corgo in the west is coolest and wettest, and vineyards get progressively hotter and drier to the east. Growers contend with spring frost and downpours that can disrupt flowering and harvest; summers are very hot and very dry.

DI: Il pestaggio a piedi è il metodo tradizionale e il riferimento: squadre di lavoratori pestano le uve per tre-quattro ore in vasche basse di granito (lagares), poi, iniziata la fermentazione, si follano regolarmente le vinacce. Oggi è poco usato perché molto laborioso, ma resta per alcuni vini premium. Negli autovinificatori la CO2 spinge il mosto in una vasca superiore e una valvola lo fa ricadere sul cappello: simile al rimontaggio, ma con molto più contatto perché in fermentazione attiva un ciclo si completa ogni 15–20 minuti; l'estrazione pre-fermentativa è limitata e i modelli nuovi hanno pale meccaniche. DF: Le foulage au pied est la méthode traditionnelle et la référence : des équipes foulent le raisin trois à quatre heures dans des cuves basses de granit (lagares), puis, une fois la fermentation lancée, on pige régulièrement le chapeau. Dans l'autovinificateur, le CO2 pousse le moût dans un réservoir supérieur et une valve le fait retomber sur le chapeau : proche du remontage, mais avec bien plus de contact car en pleine fermentation un cycle se fait toutes les 15–20 minutes ; l'extraction préfermentaire est limitée et les modèles récents ont des pales. Beaucoup les jugent aussi bons que le foulage et ils sont très utilisés pour le haut de gamme.
DE: Foot treading is the traditional method and the benchmark: teams tread grapes for three to four hours in shallow granite troughs (lagares), then, once fermentation starts, the cap is regularly punched down. It is little used now as it is so labour-intensive, but remains for some premium wines. In autovinifiers CO2 pushes juice into a holding tank and a valve releases it over the cap: like pumping over, but with far more contact as a cycle completes every 15–20 minutes in active fermentation; pre-fermentation extraction is limited, so newer models have paddles. Piston plungers press the cap in round, shallow, open-topped stainless vats; the robotic lagar is a rectangular stainless tank with a self-propelled gantry whose robotic 'feet' squash grapes against the floor, then punch down. Many consider these as good as treading, and they are widely used for premium wines.
