Q: Bordeaux: Rive Sinistra e Rive Destra. || Bordeaux : rive gauche et rive droite. || Bordeaux: Left Bank and Right Bank.
P6: Spiega perché il Cabernet Sauvignon domina nel Médoc e il Merlot a Saint-Émilion e Pomerol. || Expliquez pourquoi le cabernet-sauvignon domine dans le Médoc et le merlot à Saint-Émilion et Pomerol. || Explain why Cabernet Sauvignon dominates the Médoc and Merlot dominates Saint-Émilion and Pomerol.
K: moderate maritime climate, Gulf Stream extends season (ripening into October, few spring frosts); Landes forest and dunes shelter from storms; Cabernet late ripening: high stone/gravel soils of Haut-Médoc, Bas-Médoc and Graves raise vineyard temperature → only areas where it reliably ripens (up to three-quarters of top blends); Bas-Médoc more clay → more Merlot, earlier drinking; Merlot grows on cooler clay soils of Saint-Émilion and Pomerol where Cabernet would struggle; Saint-Émilion plateau (gravel and limestone, some Cabernet Franc/Sauvignon), clay-limestone escarpment (most prestigious), sandy foot (lighter); Left Bank: blackcurrant, cedar, grippy young tannins, long ageing; Right Bank: medium-high tannin but soft rich mouthfeel, red berry, plum, tobacco and cedar with age; Pomerol richer, spicier blackberry; Pessac-Léognan reds slightly lighter and more fragrant
P6: Perché il taglio è fondamentale a Bordeaux e che ruolo hanno Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Cabernet Franc e Petit Verdot? || Pourquoi l'assemblage est-il essentiel à Bordeaux et quel rôle jouent cabernet-sauvignon, merlot, cabernet franc et petit verdot ? || Why is blending essential in Bordeaux, and what do Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot contribute?
K: rain throughout year disrupts flowering/fruit set, promotes rot, dilutes at harvest → vintage variation; varieties flower and ripen at different times, so one frost or shower won't ruin the crop; 13 permitted, three black and two white predominate; Cabernet Sauvignon: colour, tannin, blackcurrant, structure, ageing; Merlot: most planted, softness, dominant in inexpensive wines; Cabernet Franc: less body and tannin, vibrant fruit and floral notes when ripe, herbaceous/stalky if unripe, prefers warm drained soils (Saint-Émilion); Petit Verdot: small plantings, ripens fully only in very hot years, minor role adding tannin, colour, spice; blends made the spring after vintage or kept separate; second and third wines protect the grand vin; better canopy management → healthier, riper grapes

Q: Gerarchia e stili dei vini di Bordeaux. || Hiérarchie et styles des vins de Bordeaux. || Bordeaux hierarchy and styles.
P5: Descrivi la differenza tra un Bordeaux AC di base e un Grand Cru Classé del Médoc in vigneto e in cantina. || Décrivez la différence entre un Bordeaux AC basique et un grand cru classé du Médoc à la vigne et à la cave. || Describe the difference between a basic Bordeaux AC and a Médoc Grand Cru Classé in the vineyard and winery.
K: generic Bordeaux / Bordeaux Supérieur (stricter, higher alcohol), mainly red, Merlot-led, unlikely to see oak; best are early-drinking medium-bodied, ripe red and black fruit, sometimes cedar; cheapest lighter, more astringent; improving to compete with world wines; classified château: Haut-Médoc communes (Saint-Estèphe, Pauillac, Saint-Julien, Margaux), gravel, high Cabernet, rejection of unhealthy/unripe grapes, sorting, varied vats (oak, steel, concrete) for blending options, second/third wines, 225 L barriques all new at finest; blackcurrant, cedar, grippy tannins, long ageing; prices reflect huge gap in resources between wealthy few and most producers
P5: Come si producono i bianchi secchi di Pessac-Léognan e i dolci di Sauternes? || Comment produit-on les blancs secs de Pessac-Léognan et les liquoreux de Sauternes ? || How are dry Pessac-Léognan whites and sweet Sauternes made?
K: dry whites: Sauvignon Blanc (citrus, green fruit, acidity; increasingly varietal; generic white Bordeaux grassy), Semillon adds body, Muscadelle small amounts grapey floral; basic fresh fruity in inert vessels; Pessac-Léognan Sauvignon-Semillon fermented/matured partly in new oak: medium-full body, rich nutty toasty; Graves and Entre-Deux-Mers usually unoaked Sauvignon; Médoc/Sauternes dry whites sold as Bordeaux; Sauternes/Barsac: Garonne and Ciron create misty autumns for noble rot on fully ripe grapes; level varies by year, passerillage helps in low-rot years; Semillon dominates (thin skin, botrytis-prone), Sauvignon acidity and fruit, Muscadelle exotic perfume; selective hand picking; best fermented/matured in new oak up to three years; high alcohol, sweet balanced by high acidity, apricot, citrus peel, toast, vanilla

Q: Il Sud-Ovest della Francia. || Le Sud-Ouest de la France. || South West France.
P5: Quali uve e stili caratterizzano Bergerac/Monbazillac, Cahors, Madiran e Jurançon? || Quels cépages et styles caractérisent Bergerac/Monbazillac, Cahors, Madiran et Jurançon ? || What grapes and styles characterise Bergerac/Monbazillac, Cahors, Madiran and Jurançon?
K: Dordogne: climate like Bordeaux but less maritime; Bergerac reds and whites from Bordeaux varieties; Monbazillac good-value botrytised sweet Semillon and Sauvignon; Cahors (either side of the Lot): historically 'black' very tannic long-lived; Malbec most important, plus Merlot and Tannat; styles vary by valley floor, slopes, plateau; best oak-aged, deep colour, high tannin, intense dark berries, mature to cedar and earth; Madiran: Tannat-led blends, deep colour, high tannin, traditionally long bottle ageing, now riper grapes and modern winemaking → concentrated black fruit, softer riper tannins; Jurançon (Pyrenees foothills): dry and sweet whites; best sweet from high-acid Petit Manseng, passerillage without botrytis, pronounced apricot and grapefruit, spice from new oak
P5: Che cosa produce l'IGP Côtes de Gascogne e perché i vini del Sud-Ovest differiscono da quelli di Bordeaux? || Que produit l'IGP Côtes de Gascogne et pourquoi les vins du Sud-Ouest diffèrent-ils de ceux de Bordeaux ? || What does Côtes de Gascogne IGP produce, and why do South West wines differ from Bordeaux?
K: considerable IGP production south-east of Bordeaux; Côtes de Gascogne: generally dry, light-bodied whites from Ugni Blanc, green apple; South West regions use varieties rarely seen in Bordeaux (Malbec, Tannat, Petit Manseng) with different styles; inland/south, less maritime; Dordogne uses the same varieties as Bordeaux

Q: Le classificazioni di Bordeaux. || Les classements de Bordeaux. || The Bordeaux classifications.
P6: Confronta la classificazione del 1855, quella delle Graves, quella di Saint-Émilion e i Cru Bourgeois. || Comparez le classement de 1855, celui des Graves, celui de Saint-Émilion et les crus bourgeois. || Compare the 1855 classification, the Graves classification, Saint-Émilion's and the Crus Bourgeois.
K: except Saint-Émilion, classifications are outside the appellation system and rank châteaux (estates), not vineyards — château names are brands as plots are bought and sold; 1855 for Paris Universal Exhibition by Chamber of Commerce: Médoc reds in five ranks (top: Lafite Rothschild, Latour, Margaux, Mouton Rothschild + Haut-Brion from Graves); Sauternes three ranks with Yquem on top; virtually intact, debate on current quality; Graves: separate lists for reds and whites, no ranking, all crus classés, now within Pessac-Léognan; Saint-Émilion: integrated in appellation (Saint-Émilion Grand Cru; Grand Cru Classé; Premier Grand Cru Classé B and A), revised at least every ten years with promotion/demotion; Cru Bourgeois for other Médoc châteaux, now awarded to a specific vintage, wines submitted yearly; vins de garage tiny-plot very ripe Right Bank wines, some now classified; Côtes de Bordeaux group (Blaye, Cadillac, Castillon, Francs) Merlot-based value, not Côtes de Bourg; Premières Côtes de Bordeaux sweet
