Q: Borgogna: terroir e classificazione. || Bourgogne : terroir et classement. || Burgundy: terroir and classification.
P6: Descrivi il clima e i fattori di sito della Côte d'Or e perché i migliori vigneti sono a mezza costa. || Décrivez le climat et les facteurs de site de la Côte d'Or et pourquoi les meilleures vignes sont à mi-pente. || Describe the Côte d'Or's climate and site factors, and why the best vineyards lie mid-slope.
K: cool continental (Chablis) to moderate continental south; rain disrupts flowering and harvest; Pinot susceptible to grey rot; spring frosts (Chablis, as late as May), localised summer hail; village vineyards on flat land or slope bottoms, premiers/grands crus mid-slope: less frost, south or east facing, protection from westerly winds; Massif Central gives east/south-east facing slopes; hillside soils shallower, better drained; flat land deeper and more fertile; soils vary greatly over small areas (faults, erosion), role of soil debated; VSP lets light into canopy
P6: Spiega la gerarchia delle AC di Borgogna e il ruolo dei négociants rispetto ai domaines. || Expliquez la hiérarchie des AC de Bourgogne et le rôle des négociants face aux domaines. || Explain Burgundy's AC hierarchy and the role of négociants versus domaines.
K: hierarchy based on vineyard quality; regional (about half of production): Bourgogne Rouge/Blanc from anywhere, usually Pinot and Chardonnay; more restricted regional: Bourgogne Hautes Côtes de Nuits/Beaune, Bourgogne Côte Chalonnaise, Mâcon, Mâcon Villages; some declassified from higher appellations (yields or prestige); commune/village (about a third), e.g. Chablis, Gevrey-Chambertin, single non-classified vineyard name may appear; premier cru (more than 600, about a tenth): village + 'premier cru' + vineyard name, or blend of premiers crus without vineyard name; grand cru (33: 32 Côte d'Or, 1 Chablis; little more than 1%): vineyard name only plus 'grand cru'; most vineyards divided among many owners, monopole = single owner; Gamay only regional, village and above Pinot only for reds; négociants (merchants) buy grapes, juice or wine and blend, domaines (estates) make wine only from own grapes, greatest control but fewer blending options in bad years

Q: Stili e zone della Borgogna e del Beaujolais. || Styles et zones de Bourgogne et du Beaujolais. || Burgundy and Beaujolais styles and areas.
P6: Confronta lo Chardonnay di Chablis, della Côte de Beaune e del Mâconnais. || Comparez le chardonnay de Chablis, de la Côte de Beaune et du Mâconnais. || Compare Chardonnay from Chablis, the Côte de Beaune and the Mâconnais.
K: Chablis: Serein valley, only Chardonnay, frost (sprinklers, heaters); Petit Chablis and Chablis on north-facing/flatter land: austere, green fruit, high acidity; premiers/grands crus on south-facing hillsides: riper citrus, more body, still high acidity; some old oak for rounder texture, others steel/concrete for pure fruit; MLF softens sharp acidity; Côte de Beaune (Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet, Chassagne-Montrachet, Corton-Charlemagne): barrel fermentation, 6–9 months barrel ageing, MLF, lees, debated new oak/toast; complex, expressive, stone fruit with creamy oak, age a decade+ (nuts, mushroom); premature oxidation problem; Mâconnais: warmer south, Mâcon fresh apple/citrus, medium acidity, medium-full body, hint of MLF creaminess; Mâcon Villages riper, value (Lugny); Pouilly-Fuissé, Saint-Véran on limestone Roche de Solutré amphitheatre suntraps: ripest richest, tropical and stone fruit, barrel maturation, toasty
P4: Perché il Gamay e la macerazione carbonica caratterizzano il Beaujolais, e che cosa distingue i Cru? || Pourquoi le gamay et la macération carbonique caractérisent-ils le Beaujolais, et qu'est-ce qui distingue les crus ? || Why do Gamay and carbonic maceration characterise Beaujolais, and what distinguishes the Crus?
K: climate like southern Mâconnais suits early budding/ripening Gamay; can give large yields → granite low-nutrient soils limit yields, concentrate flavour; gobelet pruning (shoots tied), increasingly wires for mechanisation; fragrant raspberry, cherry, rarely above medium tannin/body, lightest can be chilled; hierarchy: Beaujolais (Saône alluvial plain, carbonic/semi-carbonic, light, kirsch, banana, cinnamon), Beaujolais Nouveau (third Thursday of November, sold by 31 August, only Beaujolais or Villages quality), Beaujolais Villages (39 villages on granite hills, usually blends), 10 crus (Brouilly, Morgon, Fleurie, Moulin-à-Vent largest; Moulin-à-Vent and Morgon most structured, can age; Brouilly and Fleurie lighter, perfumed); crus often crushed-fruit fermentation, some large vats not new oak, or semi-carbonic/whole bunches for brighter fruit

Q: Côte de Nuits e Côte Chalonnaise. || Côte de Nuits et Côte chalonnaise. || Côte de Nuits and Côte Chalonnaise.
P6: Che cosa distingue Côte de Nuits, Côte de Beaune e Côte Chalonnaise per stili e villaggi principali? || Qu'est-ce qui distingue Côte de Nuits, Côte de Beaune et Côte chalonnaise en styles et villages principaux ? || What distinguishes the Côte de Nuits, Côte de Beaune and Côte Chalonnaise in styles and main villages?
K: Côte de Nuits: fullest-bodied, longest-lived Pinot Noir; all red grands crus but one (Corton); villages N→S Gevrey-Chambertin (Chambertin, Clos de Bèze), Vougeot (Clos de Vougeot), Vosne-Romanée (Romanée-Conti, La Tâche, La Romanée), Nuits-Saint-Georges; Côte de Nuits-Villages red or white; Côte de Beaune: fruitier reds, most famous for Chardonnay, all white grands crus but one (Chablis); villages Aloxe-Corton (Corton, Corton-Charlemagne), Beaune, Pommard and Volnay (red only), Meursault, Puligny- and Chassagne-Montrachet (Montrachet); Côte de Beaune-Villages red; Hautes Côtes higher, windier, cooler, less body; Côte Chalonnaise: higher altitude, later and less reliable ripening, less consistent east aspect → lighter, earlier maturing, less prestigious; Rully (more white, sparkling centre), Mercurey (best reds), Givry (smallest, admired reds), Montagny (white only); premiers crus but no grands crus; Pinot red typically red fruit → earth, game, mushroom, high acid, low-medium tannin, 16–18 months barrel for best, whole bunches more popular
