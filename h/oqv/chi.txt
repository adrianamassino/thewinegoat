P6: Spiega come la corrente di Humboldt, la catena costiera e le Ande influenzano i vigneti cileni, e il significato di Costa, Entre Cordilleras e Andes. || Expliquez l'influence du courant de Humboldt, de la cordillère côtière et des Andes sur les vignes chiliennes, et le sens de Costa, Entre Cordilleras et Andes. || Explain how the Humboldt Current, the Coastal Range and the Andes affect Chilean vineyards, and the meaning of Costa, Entre Cordilleras and Andes.
K: over 900 km north-south, rarely over 100 km wide; Pacific, coastal ranges, Andes (border), Central Valley depression between ranges south of Santiago; river-valley gaps in coastal range; warm Mediterranean, dry sunny season → reliable ripening, little fungal disease; irrigation but water increasingly short; cooler/wetter in south; El Niño more rain, La Niña drought; cold Humboldt Current from Antarctica, prevailing winds blow cool air inland along river valleys, fog near coast; strongest where coastal mountains lowest and closest to Pacific; Andes foothills: cold air descends at night → large diurnal range; between ranges more sheltered, flat, easy to cultivate (warm); these two cooling influences explain 2012 labelling terms Costa (coastal), Entre Cordilleras (between ranges), Andes (foothills), added to region name; uncertain whether they will become widely used; Reserva etc. carry little overall meaning
P4: Descrivi Carmenère e Cabernet Sauvignon cileni e dove eccellono. || Décrivez carmenère et cabernet-sauvignon chiliens et où ils excellent. || Describe Chilean Carmenère and Cabernet Sauvignon and where they excel.
K: black varieties majority; Cabernet most planted of either colour, simple fruity to full premium; very ripe black fruit, often herbaceous; blended with Merlot, Carmenère or Syrah; Maipo (minty, Andean foothills, elegance and structure), Colchagua (full), cooler east Cachapoal, Aconcagua; Carmenère: late ripening, warmest sunniest sites (e.g. Cachapoal valley floor, Colchagua, Aconcagua); full body, often high tannin; overtly herbaceous if not fully ripe; good examples balance black fruit and herbal; Chile's signature grape; Merlot medium-bodied fruity successful on export; Syrah lighter peppery in cool (Elqui, Casablanca) vs fuller black fruit in hot (Colchagua)

Q: Argentina. || Argentine. || Argentina.
P6: Spiega perché l'altitudine è il fattore chiave in Argentina e come cambia il Malbec da Luján de Cuyo alla Uco Valley. || Expliquez pourquoi l'altitude est le facteur clé en Argentine et comment le malbec change de Luján de Cuyo à la vallée d'Uco. || Explain why altitude is the key factor in Argentina and how Malbec changes from Luján de Cuyo to the Uco Valley.
K: cut off from Pacific by Andes; irrigated oases in desert close to Andes, over 1500 km Salta to Río Negro/Neuquén; most vineyards 600 m+, Salta (Cafayate) 2000 m+ → cool sites close to Equator; best often over 1000 m; parral pergola traditionally lifted grapes from ground heat and shaded fruit, still for Torrontés; black grapes now vertical trellis with canopy shading; rain shadow → extremely low rain; water from rivers/aquifers; flood irrigation → drip in newer vineyards; spring frost occasional, summer hail regular esp. Mendoza (netting, vineyards in several areas); dry → little rot/mildew; Malbec flagship, largest plantings worldwide: deep, full, black fruit, high smooth tannins; gentler extraction for elegance; best new oak, subtle spice; lower altitude fuller, richer black fruit; higher altitude more elegant, fresher, more floral; Luján de Cuyo (Central Mendoza, 900–1100 m, Andes foothills): very fine Malbec, old vines give soft, round, sweet spice in blends; Uco Valley 900–1500 m, highest in Mendoza, cool nights retain acidity and fresh fruit, floral notes; Tupungato; Malbec varietal or with Bordeaux varieties
P4: Descrivi il Torrontés e la provincia di Salta. || Décrivez le torrontés et la province de Salta. || Describe Torrontés and Salta province.
K: Torrontés white signature, planted in Salta, La Rioja, San Juan, Mendoza; best from high-altitude Cafayate (Salta); intense fruity, floral perfume, medium body and acidity, stone fruit and melon; aromatic → fermented in inert temperature-controlled vessels, released in year of harvest; often parral-trained; Salta: most northerly, near Bolivia, some of world's highest vineyards over 3000 m; extreme climate → great purity and concentration; Mendoza wineries and European companies invested for Torrontés; Malbec and Cabernet dominant black in Cafayate; La Rioja province third largest, Famatina Valley, Torrontés speciality, labelled Famatina to avoid confusion with Spain

Q: Argentina: vitigni e regioni oltre Mendoza. || Argentine : cépages et régions au-delà de Mendoza. || Argentina: grape varieties and regions beyond Mendoza.
P5: Descrivi Bonarda e gli altri vitigni argentini (Cabernet, Syrah, Chardonnay, Pedro Giménez). || Décrivez la bonarda et les autres cépages argentins (cabernet, syrah, chardonnay, pedro giménez). || Describe Bonarda and the other Argentine grapes (Cabernet, Syrah, Chardonnay, Pedro Giménez).
K: Bonarda second most planted, late ripening, deep colour, high acid and tannin; high yields → easy-drinking, medium-deep, raspberry and blackberry; controlled yields, full ripeness → more concentration and structure; mainly Mendoza and San Juan; old vine Bonarda in Maipú; Cabernet, Syrah, Merlot widely planted, some Tempranillo, Pinot Noir; premium concentrated ripe fruit with toasty oak; Syrah noted in San Juan, Maipú good for Syrah and Cabernet; Chardonnay range of styles, premium oak-matured with some new; Chenin, Sauvignon, Semillon, Viognier; Pedro Giménez (not Pedro Ximénez) low quality, cheap domestic wine, declining but still largest white plantings
P5: Confronta San Juan, le divisioni di Mendoza (Nord/Est, Centro, Uco, Sud) e la Patagonia. || Comparez San Juan, les divisions de Mendoza (Nord/Est, Centre, Uco, Sud) et la Patagonie. || Compare San Juan, the divisions of Mendoza (North/East, Central, Uco, South) and Patagonia.
K: GI system: three broad regions (only Patagonia often on labels) → provinces → smaller areas; San Juan second most important, 450–1400 m, hotter and more arid than Mendoza; noted Syrah; Malbec, Cabernet, Bonarda; Chardonnay, Viognier; Mendoza vast majority of production, desert, Andes rain shadow; five divisions often blended; Northern and Eastern: high-volume inexpensive, Mendoza river irrigation; Central: longest quality history, Luján de Cuyo fine Malbec, Maipú lower (Syrah, Cabernet; eastern part volume, old Bonarda); Uco Valley highest 900–1500 m, cool nights, acidity, floral; Chardonnay, Torrontés, Sauvignon, Malbec, Cabernet, Merlot, Tempranillo, some Pinot; Southern Mendoza San Rafael: low altitude but southerly → one of coolest, most significant Chenin Blanc; Patagonia (Río Negro, Neuquén): 200–250 m, cooling from latitude not altitude; strong desert winds; low rain and wide diurnal range → little disease; long days, cool nights → concentrated but fresh fruit, medium-high acid; Sauvignon, Pinot Noir, Malbec, Merlot premium
