Q: Abbinamento cibo-vino secondo il manuale. || Accords mets-vins selon le manuel. || Food and wine pairing according to the textbook.
P6: Spiega come dolcezza, umami, acidità e sale nel cibo modificano la percezione del vino. || Expliquez comment sucre, umami, acidité et sel dans le plat modifient la perception du vin. || Explain how sweetness, umami, acidity and salt in food change how a wine tastes.
K: sweetness and umami make wine 'harder': increase bitterness, astringency, acidity and warming effect of alcohol; decrease body, sweetness and fruitiness; sweet dish → choose wine with at least as much / more sugar than the dish, else wine tastes less fruity and unpleasantly acidic; umami foods lacking salt (asparagus, eggs, mushrooms, ripe soft cheeses) difficult; low-tannin reds or oaked/skin-contact whites become surprisingly bitter; umami can be balanced by adding salt or acid; cured/smoked meats and fish, Parmesan have umami plus salt so less problematic; acid and salt make wine 'softer': acidity in food increases body, sweetness, fruitiness, decreases perceived acidity (balances very high-acid wines but makes low-acid wines flat, flabby); salt increases body, decreases astringency, bitterness and acidity; food generally affects wine more than the reverse, usually negatively
P4: Come interagiscono piccante (chili), grasso/olio e intensità aromatica con il vino? || Comment interagissent piment, gras/huile et intensité aromatique avec le vin ? || How do chilli heat, fat/oil and flavour intensity interact with wine?
K: chilli heat increases bitterness, astringency, acidity and burning effect of alcohol, decreases body, richness, sweetness, fruitiness; effect greater in high-alcohol wines and alcohol increases chilli burn; choose whites or low-tannin reds not high in alcohol, with more fruit/sweetness; sensitivity and enjoyment vary greatly; acid wine with fatty/oily food: 'cuts through' richness, cleanses palate (subjective); flavour intensities usually matched so neither overpowers, but an intense dish like curry can go with a simple, non-aromatic unoaked white; bitterness in food adds to bitterness in wine; sweet and salty (sweet wine and blue cheese) subjective but popular

Q: Rischi, regole tradizionali e abbinamenti classici. || Risques, règles traditionnelles et accords classiques. || Risk, traditional rules and classic pairings.
P5: Che cosa sono cibi e vini «ad alto rischio» e «a basso rischio»? || Que sont les mets et vins « à haut risque » et « à faible risque » ? || What are 'high-risk' and 'low-risk' foods and wines?
K: high-risk foods: sugar (wine with at least as much sugar), umami (wine needs concentrated fruit to cope; or add acid/salt), bitterness (choose whites or low-tannin reds), chilli heat (whites or low-tannin reds, not high alcohol, with more fruit/sweetness); low-risk foods: high salt and/or acid (but high-acid food needs high-acid wine); high-risk wines: high bitterness and astringency from oak and grape tannins, high acidity and alcohol, complex flavours — more interactions, more interesting but harder; low-risk wines: simple, unoaked with a little residual sugar — rarely made unpleasant but change little so less interesting; people differ in sensitivity and preference
P5: Spiega perché funzionano ostriche con Muscadet o Champagne e che cosa dice il manuale della regola «rosso con la carne, bianco con il pesce» e del «perfect match». || Expliquez pourquoi huîtres avec muscadet ou champagne fonctionnent et ce que dit le manuel de la règle « rouge avec la viande, blanc avec le poisson » et de « l'accord parfait ». || Explain why oysters work with Muscadet or Champagne, and what the textbook says about 'red with meat, white with fish' and the 'perfect match'.
K: Muscadet/Champagne unoaked (no bitterness spoiled by oyster umami), light in flavour (do not overwhelm oysters), high acidity (still vibrant with lemon juice); same logic suggests Rías Baixas Albariño, Hunter Valley Semillon; red meat rule: tannins bind proteins but salt in meat dishes thought more important in softening tannins; fish umami can make red bitter but salt and acid in fish dishes counteract; oily fish + red → metallic taste, safer with white; consider whole dish and sauces; perfect match: no objectively perfect pairing because sensitivities and preferences vary; best: 'most people will find dish X goes with style Y'; flavour matching/contrasting only enhances if structural pairing works; local wine with local food works but still needs knowledge of styles
