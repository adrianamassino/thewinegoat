Q: Il Riesling nel mondo. || Le riesling dans le monde. || Riesling around the world.
P8: Confronta il Riesling di Mosella, Alsazia, Wachau e Clare/Eden Valley spiegando come clima e vinificazione determinano lo stile. || Comparez le riesling de Moselle, d'Alsace, de Wachau et de Clare/Eden en expliquant comment climat et vinification déterminent le style. || Compare Riesling from the Mosel, Alsace, the Wachau and Clare/Eden Valley, explaining how climate and winemaking shape style.
K: very tolerant of cold winters, buds late (avoids spring frost) → cool climates: green fruit, floral; warmer → richer, citrus and stone fruit, less delicacy; mid-late ripening, accumulates sugar while keeping high acidity → balanced wines dry to sweet; one of most long-lived whites: honey, toast (petrol) while keeping acidity; aromatic: protective handling, inert vessels, cool fermentation, MLF avoided, rarely blended, new oak undesirable (Alsace/Germany large old vats, a little oxidation); sweet premium by stopping fermentation (chilling/SO2), botrytised stops naturally; Mosel: cool, steep slate slopes by river, light body, low alcohol, high acid, floral/green fruit, dry to luscious (Kabinett 8–9% sweet styles); Alsace: Vosges rain shadow, sunny dry, mainly dry Rieslings, late harvest/botrytised when vintage permits, foudres; Wachau (Austria): steep terraced south-facing by Danube, dry, medium-full, ripe peachy, ages; Austria mostly dry or very sweet; Clare (cool breezes, cold nights, altitude) and Eden (altitude): bone dry, high acid, lime, citrus; toast, honey, petrol develop rapidly; NZ and Finger Lakes off-dry; Washington dry

Q: Lo Chardonnay nel mondo. || Le chardonnay dans le monde. || Chardonnay around the world.
P8: Spiega come la stessa uva dia uno Chablis, un Meursault, un Chardonnay di Napa e uno della Côte des Blancs per spumante. || Expliquez comment le même cépage donne un chablis, un meursault, un chardonnay de Napa et un chardonnay de la Côte des Blancs pour effervescent. || Explain how the same grape gives a Chablis, a Meursault, a Napa Chardonnay and a Côte des Blancs Chardonnay for sparkling wine.
K: ripens easily in many climates, early budding (spring frost); cool → green fruit, citrus; moderate → white peach, melon; warm/hot → banana, pineapple, acid lost quickly (harvest timing critical); subtle aromas → blank canvas for winemaking; options: whole-bunch pressing, controlled oxygen, some solids, barrels (new = toast, rounder), MLF (rounder, creamier, softens acid), lees ageing/stirring, blending batches; Chablis: north, cool, high acid, green apple, citrus, wet stones; steel/concrete or older larger oak, MLF almost always; Côte d'Or (Meursault): stone fruit with creamy oak, small new oak barrels, MLF, extended lees; ages to nuts/mushroom; Napa/California: valley floor warm, ripe tropical; formerly very full, high alcohol, low acid, oak, hazelnut, butter; now restrained from cooler Carneros/Russian River; New World style depends on winemaker more than tradition, moving away from heavy oak; Côte des Blancs: cool continental Champagne, low sugar high acid, lighter than Burgundy, floral, citrus; brings citrus, finesse, longevity to blends; Blanc de Blancs; autolysis from second fermentation on lees

Q: Il Pinot Noir nel mondo. || Le pinot noir dans le monde. || Pinot Noir around the world.
P8: Perché il Pinot Noir è difficile da coltivare e come cambiano gli stili tra Borgogna, Central Otago, Yarra Valley/Mornington e Oregon? || Pourquoi le pinot noir est-il difficile à cultiver et comment varient les styles entre Bourgogne, Central Otago, Yarra/Mornington et Oregon ? || Why is Pinot Noir difficult to grow, and how do styles vary between Burgundy, Central Otago, Yarra/Mornington and Oregon?
K: early budding, early ripening, thin skin; hard to grow, prefers cool-moderate climates; too hot → jammy; very old variety, many clones; colour and tannin tricky to extract, high yields unsuitable → rarely high-volume; styles light fruity red cherry to complex earthy, spice, forest floor; winemaking: destem + pre-fermentation maceration, or increasingly whole bunches (red fruit, floral), ferment often above 30°C, little post-fermentation maceration, 12–24 months oak mostly second/third fill, rarely blended; ages to forest floor; Burgundy: entry level light, marked acid; better Côte d'Or sites intense, complex, delicate/floral to tannic/spicy; cool vintages → vegetal cabbage, wet leaves; Central Otago: continental, frost, large diurnal range, very intense sunlight → high alcohol, full, juicy, vibrant ripe red fruit; Yarra: cool-moderate maritime, rich strawberry, plum, dark cherry, ripe soft tannins; Mornington: pure fruit, light to structured, vintage variation; Willamette (Oregon): moderate, Pacific breezes, dry season, cool nights → ripe red fruit, cinnamon spice, high acidity; also sparkling

Q: Syrah/Shiraz e Grenache nel mondo. || Syrah/shiraz et grenache dans le monde. || Syrah/Shiraz and Grenache around the world.
P8: Confronta Syrah dell'Hermitage/Côte-Rôtie, Shiraz della Barossa e Syrah di zone fresche (es. Elqui, Hawke's Bay), e spiega il ruolo della Grenache nei tagli del Rodano meridionale, di Priorat e della Barossa. || Comparez la syrah d'Hermitage/Côte-Rôtie, la shiraz de Barossa et la syrah de zones fraîches (Elqui, Hawke's Bay), et expliquez le rôle du grenache dans les assemblages du Rhône sud, du Priorat et de Barossa. || Compare Hermitage/Côte-Rôtie Syrah, Barossa Shiraz and cool-area Syrah (e.g. Elqui, Hawke's Bay), and explain Grenache's role in Southern Rhône, Priorat and Barossa blends.
K: Syrah small berries, thick dark skins, will not ripen in very cool climates; medium-bodied pepper and fresh black fruit to full, very ripe black fruit, liquorice; deep colour, high tannin → best age; Northern Rhône at coolest limit: lesser sites light, simple, herbaceous, grippy; south-facing slopes (Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage) fuller, berry, pepper, meat and leather with age; Barossa: warm, old bush vines, full, soft tannins, ripe black fruit, sweet American oak, leather/spice with age; warm-climate winemaking: vigorous extraction, new oak; restrained styles: earlier picking, gentler cap management, whole bunches, post-fermentation maceration, larger older barrels; cool areas (Elqui, Great Southern, Geelong, Heathcote) leaner, more peppery; NZ Syrah elegant, closer to Rhône; Grenache late ripening, needs warm/hot, drought tolerant; sweet thin-skinned → high alcohol, low acid, full, soft tannins; rarely varietal; jammy in heat, so blended with Carignan/Mourvèdre for fresher fruit, tannin, colour; old oak large vessels; Southern Rhône most planted, Châteauneuf-du-Pape with Syrah and Mourvèdre: full, rich, spicy red fruit; Priorat with Cariñena: deep, high tannin, black fruit, toasty oak; Rioja Baja: perfume, body, alcohol to Tempranillo; Barossa/McLaren Vale old bush vines: concentrated red berry, peppery spice, now more restraint

Q: Cabernet Sauvignon e Merlot nel mondo. || Cabernet-sauvignon et merlot dans le monde. || Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot around the world.
P8: Confronta il Cabernet/i tagli bordolesi di Médoc, Napa, Coonawarra, Stellenbosch e Maipo/Colchagua. || Comparez le cabernet/les assemblages bordelais du Médoc, de Napa, de Coonawarra, de Stellenbosch et de Maipo/Colchagua. || Compare Cabernet/Bordeaux blends from the Médoc, Napa, Coonawarra, Stellenbosch and Maipo/Colchagua.
K: Cabernet thick-skinned → colour, flavour, tannin; late ripening, in cool places may not ripen in poor weather → astringent tannins, herbaceous; destemmed (stems herbaceous/astringent), pre-fermentation maceration, 26–30°C, avoid harsh extraction at end, post-fermentation maceration to soften; suits new French barriques 12–18 months and bottle ageing; on its own high tannin, can lack body → blended with Merlot (softness, fruit), Shiraz in Australia; Haut-Médoc: maritime, gravel, long-lived, grippy tannins young, cedar, blackcurrant leaf; softens with age; outside Europe: warmth and bright sun → full, ripe blackcurrant, black cherry, smooth tannins (Napa, Coonawarra, Margaret River, Hawke's Bay, Stellenbosch, Colchagua); Napa valley floor full, powerful (Rutherford), sides more tannic; Coonawarra terra rossa over limestone, moderate maritime, concentrated, cassis, eucalyptus/menthol; Stellenbosch False Bay breezes, Bordeaux blends; Maipo minty, Andean foothills elegance; Colchagua full; Merlot buds and ripens earlier, juicy plum, smoother texture; dominant in Saint-Émilion and Pomerol; international style (late harvest, purple, concentrated blackberry, plum, velvety tannins, new oak) vs earlier-picked Bordeaux style (medium body, higher acid, fresh red fruit, leafy)

Q: Sauvignon Blanc e Chenin Blanc nel mondo. || Sauvignon blanc et chenin blanc dans le monde. || Sauvignon Blanc and Chenin Blanc around the world.
P8: Confronta il Sauvignon Blanc di Sancerre, Marlborough, Bordeaux (Pessac-Léognan) e Casablanca, e il Chenin della Loira e del Sudafrica. || Comparez le sauvignon de Sancerre, Marlborough, Bordeaux (Pessac-Léognan) et Casablanca, et le chenin de Loire et d'Afrique du Sud. || Compare Sauvignon Blanc from Sancerre, Marlborough, Bordeaux (Pessac-Léognan) and Casablanca, and Chenin from the Loire and South Africa.
K: Sauvignon highly aromatic, high acid, early ripening → suits cool climates; vigorous (high yields, herbaceous if unripe); protective handling, inert vessels, cool fermentation, dry, no MLF, drink young; Sancerre/Pouilly-Fumé: cool, elegance and restraint, green apple, asparagus, wet pebbles; Marlborough: cool but long intense sunlight → vibrant, gooseberry, elderflower, grapefruit, passion fruit, some herbaceous; Pessac-Léognan: barrel fermented/matured with some new oak, lees, MLF, ambient yeasts, blended with Sémillon → rounder, spicy toasty, creamy (also California Fumé Blanc); Casablanca/San Antonio (Leyda): morning fog, afternoon winds, cool, high acid, intense fruit; Margaret River blended with Semillon; Chenin: Loire high acidity, dry to sweet and sparkling (Vouvray mostly Chenin, Savennières, Coteaux du Layon botrytis); sparkling Chenin less bready, smoky/toasty autolysis; South Africa: most planted variety; basic easy simple stone fruit; old bush vines concentrated, complex, fuller; barrel fermentation and ageing; Swartland old-vine Chenin and white blends

Q: Vini dolci nel mondo. || Vins doux dans le monde. || Sweet wines around the world.
P8: Confronta tre metodi per concentrare lo zucchero nelle uve (muffa nobile, appassimento, congelamento) con esempi di vini e loro caratteristiche. || Comparez trois méthodes de concentration du sucre (pourriture noble, passerillage, gel) avec exemples de vins et leurs caractéristiques. || Compare three ways of concentrating sugar in grapes (noble rot, drying, freezing) with example wines and their characteristics.
K: sweet wines contain unfermented sugar: stopping fermentation (fortification, chilling/SO2 + filtration, e.g. Asti, sweet Riesling), adding sweet component (sweet reserve/RCGM), or concentrating grape sugar; noble rot (Botrytis cinerea, same fungus as grey rot): fully ripe grapes, humid misty mornings then warm sunny afternoons; fungus pierces skin, water evaporates, sugar (and acid) concentrated; spread never uniform → several pickings, expensive, not every year; flavours honey, dried fruit, citrus peel (marmalade); Sauternes, Tokaji Aszú, BA/TBA, Coteaux du Layon, Neusiedlersee, Riverina Semillon; drying on vine (late harvest, passerillage: Jurançon, VT in Alsace) → concentration, raisin; drying after picking: healthy grapes dried in warm dry conditions, rotten removed (Recioto, Amarone, PX sun-dried, Santorini Vinsanto, Strohwein) → raisin, dried fruit; freezing on vine: water in pulp turns to ice, pressed frozen, concentrated juice (Eiswein, Icewine: Riesling, Vidal) → very pure varietal fruit, high acidity, no botrytis character; sweetness balanced by acidity in quality wines

Q: Rese e qualità. || Rendements et qualité. || Yields and quality.
P6: Spiega perché rese basse sono spesso associate a qualità più alta e quando invece rese più alte sono accettabili. || Expliquez pourquoi des rendements bas sont souvent liés à une qualité plus élevée et quand des rendements plus élevés sont acceptables. || Explain why low yields are often linked with higher quality, and when higher yields are acceptable.
K: KEY POINT: the textbook says that although traditionally high quality was thought to need low yields, it is in fact very hard to make any solid link between yield and quality; relationship depends on many site factors (credit a candidate who challenges the premise); yield measured in tonnes/ha or hl/ha; producers must manage and predict yields: legal limits (e.g. EU), contracts, tank space; estimate from buds after pruning but frost, poor fruit set, pests and disease reduce; green harvesting after veraison to cut yield is risky: vine compensates with bigger berries, restoring yield and diluting flavour; old vines thought to give exceptional concentrated fruit but lower yields and more disease → balance quality with profitability; low-density planting with multiple cordons/canes on fertile sites can give good quality at high yields; grapes like Cabernet, Merlot, Syrah, Grenache give fruity wines with adequate colour even at high yields, whereas Pinot Noir and Pinot Grigio (neutral) suffer; examples of small yields contributing to intensity: Priorat old vines on poor soils

Q: Vinificazione in bianco: aromatici e non aromatici. || Vinification en blanc : aromatiques et non aromatiques. || White winemaking: aromatic and non-aromatic grapes.
P6: Confronta le scelte di vinificazione per uve aromatiche (Sauvignon, Riesling) e non aromatiche (Chardonnay, Pinot Gris/Grigio), compreso Pinot Grigio economico vs premium. || Comparez les choix de vinification pour cépages aromatiques (sauvignon, riesling) et non aromatiques (chardonnay, pinot gris/grigio), y compris pinot grigio bon marché vs premium. || Compare winemaking choices for aromatic (Sauvignon, Riesling) and non-aromatic (Chardonnay, Pinot Gris/Grigio) grapes, including inexpensive versus premium Pinot Grigio.
K: general options: skin contact (little, to limit oxidation; short cool contact for some aromatics), juice clarification (settling, centrifuge, fining, filtration; some solids add complexity, risk off-flavours), fermentation 12–22°C (too cold → pear drop, warmer → non-fruit complexity but loss of varietal), steel vs barrel; aromatics: keep primary fruit, SO2 monitored, gentle settling, inert vessels, cool slow ferment, MLF blocked, little post-fermentation work, bottled early, no new oak; non-aromatics: blank canvas, controlled oxygen at pressing for ageing, some solids, barrels (new oak toast), MLF (rounder, creamier, lower perceived acid), extended lees and lees stirring, batch blending; Pinot Gris/Grigio: early budding/ripening, high sugar low acid if left; Alsace dry/off-dry, oily texture, tropical, ginger, honey, golden colour, old large oak; NZ similar viscous, purer fruit, more residual sugar; north-east Italy (Alto Adige, Trentino, Friuli) dry, greater depth, small-berried German/French clones; Veneto plain inexpensive: pale-skinned large-pulp clone, high yields, early harvest → neutral, light pear drop, light body, medium acid; high-volume whites: protective, acidification, highly clarified, cool steel, commercial yeast, MLF blocked, chips/staves, residual sugar added (juice/RCGM), sterile filtered

Q: Vinificazione in rosso e rosato: scelte di estrazione. || Vinification en rouge et rosé : choix d'extraction. || Red and rosé winemaking: extraction choices.
P6: Spiega le principali tecniche di estrazione nella vinificazione in rosso e come cambiano tra vini economici e premium. || Expliquez les principales techniques d'extraction en vinification rouge et comment elles varient entre vins bon marché et premium. || Explain the main extraction techniques in red winemaking and how they differ between inexpensive and premium wines.
K: skins in fermenter for colour and tannin; pressing after fermentation; MLF standard; most fruit destemmed and crushed; cold soak extracts colour and flavour (tannins need alcohol); fermentation 20–32°C, not above 35°C (kills yeast); lower temperature at end reduces tannin; cap management: punching down (very effective, risk of over-extraction, bitter astringent), pumping over (dissipates heat, oxygenates), rack and return (very extractive, once or twice, cools), rotary fermenters; large open-topped vessels (oak, concrete, steel), not barrels; post-fermentation maceration extracts tannin, long ones may smooth; free run vs press wine (press fractions deeper, more tannic, used to adjust); whole bunch: ripe stems needed; carbonic maceration (CO2, intracellular fermentation to ~2% abv, colour but little tannin, kirsch, banana, bubble gum, cinnamon), semi-carbonic, whole bunches with crushed fruit (silkier, fresher); oak: trend away from all-new small barrels; inexpensive: warm-climate Cabernet, Merlot, Syrah, Grenache (colour even at high yields), protective, tartaric acid additions, commercial yeast 22–25°C, cap not heavily worked, no post-fermentation maceration, (semi-)carbonic for Grenache and basic Beaujolais, short oak, second/third fill or staves/chips, stabilised and sterile filtered
