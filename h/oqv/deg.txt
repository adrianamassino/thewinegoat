Q: Spiega come valutare la qualità di un vino con il metodo SAT. || Expliquez comment évaluer la qualité d'un vin avec la méthode SAT. || Explain how to assess the quality of a wine using the SAT.
P6: Quali sono i quattro criteri (BLIC) e che cosa significa ciascuno? || Quels sont les quatre critères (BLIC) et que signifie chacun ? || What are the four criteria (BLIC) and what does each mean?
K: Balance: scales with fruit and sugar on one side, acidity and tannin on the other; too little fruit/sugar → angular, austere, thin; too little acid/tannin → unstructured, clumsy; also integration of each component (alcohol, oak) — three questions: how is balance achieved, how well balanced, how well integrated; Intensity (concentration): weak dilute flavours seldom high quality, but beyond a point more intensity is not more quality; Length: how long the pleasant sensations linger after swallowing/spitting (only desirable sensations count); intense wines usually also long; Complexity: range of aromas/flavours, from primary alone or combined with secondary and tertiary; simplicity not always negative — purity can make a wine great (e.g. Icewine)
P4: Come si usano per arrivare al giudizio di qualità e come si valuta la prontezza (readiness for drinking)? || Comment s'en sert-on pour conclure sur la qualité et comment juge-t-on l'aptitude au vieillissement ? || How are they used to reach a quality conclusion, and how is readiness for drinking judged?
K: outstanding = positive on all four criteria; very good = three (e.g. short finish); good = two; acceptable = one (e.g. balanced but dilute, simple, short); poor = problems on all four; faulty; readiness: first ask whether style can benefit from ageing; mainly primary with light acid/tannin → drink now: not suitable for ageing (or too old if it has lost freshness); firm acid/tannin structure with enough flavour concentration may benefit; with age aromas move from primary to tertiary and tannins soften; alcohol does not change, acid and sugar change very little (sweet wines slowly taste drier); categories: too young, can drink now but has potential for ageing, drink now: not suitable for ageing or further ageing, too old; in the exam reasons are not required but the factors must be understood

Q: Il SAT descrive aspetto, naso e palato. || La SAT décrit l'aspect, le nez et la bouche. || The SAT describes appearance, nose and palate.
P4: Che cosa può indicare il colore di un vino bianco e di un vino rosso riguardo a età e vinificazione? || Que peut indiquer la couleur d'un blanc et d'un rouge sur l'âge et la vinification ? || What can colour indicate about age and winemaking for white and red wines?
K: colour = balance of red, blue, yellow, green, brown, independent of intensity; white scale lemon-green, lemon (most common), gold (hint of orange/brown), amber, brown — amber/brown usually very old or deliberately oxidised wines; red scale purple (blue/purple), ruby (most common), garnet (orange/brown but still more red), tawny (more brown than red), brown (no redness) — tawny/brown usually very old or deliberately oxidised; rosé pink, salmon, orange; whites/rosés judged at core, deep reds near rim; intensity pale/medium/deep judged core-to-rim at 45° or stem visibility; oxidation fault makes wine deeper and browner than it should be
P6: Distingui aromi primari, secondari e terziari con due esempi ciascuno. || Distinguez arômes primaires, secondaires et tertiaires avec deux exemples chacun. || Distinguish primary, secondary and tertiary aromas with two examples each.
K: primary: aromas existing after fermentation, from grapes and fermentation: floral (acacia, rose, violet, elderflower), green/citrus/stone/tropical/red/black fruit, dried/cooked fruit, herbaceous (green bell pepper, grass), herbal (mint, eucalyptus), pungent spice (black/white pepper, liquorice), other (flint, wet stones, wet wool); secondary: post-fermentation winemaking: yeast/lees/autolysis (biscuit, bread, toast, brioche), MLF (butter, cream, cheese), oak (vanilla, cloves, coconut, toast, cedar, smoke); tertiary: ageing — oxidative (almond, marzipan, hazelnut, walnut, coffee, toffee, caramel) or bottle age (white: petrol, honey, toast, mushroom; red: leather, forest floor, earth, mushroom, game, tobacco, wet leaves); fruit development (dried apricot, marmalade, fig, prune); careful: dried/cooked fruit can be in young wines from dried grapes or hot climates

Q: Valutare la struttura al palato con il SAT. || Évaluer la structure en bouche avec la SAT. || Assessing structure on the palate with the SAT.
P6: Come si distinguono correttamente acidità, alcol e tannino, evitando le confusioni tipiche? || Comment distinguer correctement acidité, alcool et tanin en évitant les confusions classiques ? || How do you correctly distinguish acidity, alcohol and tannin, avoiding the usual confusions?
K: acidity: felt at sides of tongue, sharp tingling, mouth-watering — how much and how long the mouth waters is the reliable guide; sweetness and acidity mask each other, so in sweet wines acidity seems less obvious; alcohol creates burning similar to acidity — check mouth-watering (acid) vs thick, viscous (alcohol); alcohol low <11%, medium 11–13.9%, high ≥14% (fortified: low 15–16.4, medium 16.5–18.4, high ≥18.5); tannin: binds saliva, drying, rough, felt on gums above front teeth, can be bitter at back; unripe tannins more astringent, ripe tannins add textural richness — basic barely ripe Pinot can be medium tannin though harsh; hot-climate Shiraz high but velvety; astringent + thin body suggests low tannin; low astringency + full mouth-filling suggests high tannin; assess actual not apparent levels; alcohol and fruit add to perceived sweetness
P4: Che cos'è il corpo, da che cosa dipende, e come si valutano finale e mousse? || Qu'est-ce que le corps, de quoi dépend-il, et comment évaluer finale et mousse ? || What is body, what does it depend on, and how are finish and mousse assessed?
K: body/mouthfeel: overall textural impression of all structural components; alcohol main factor; sugar adds body; high acidity makes it feel lighter; high tannin fuller, but low levels of astringent tannin can feel thinner; finish: length of desirable sensations only after swallowing/spitting (basic wines a few seconds = short; very fine a minute or more = long); lingering bitterness does not count; mousse only for sparkling: creamy for most, aggressive (explodes then gone, some young wines), delicate (long aged or lower pressure)

Q: Condizione, intensità e sviluppo al naso. || Condition, intensité et évolution au nez. || Condition, intensity and development on the nose.
P6: Descrivi i principali difetti (condition) che il manuale chiede di riconoscere. || Décrivez les principaux défauts (condition) que le manuel demande de reconnaître. || Describe the main faults (condition) the textbook asks you to recognise.
K: faults not assessed in L3 tasting exam but must be known; TCA: damp cardboard, muted fruit, often from tainted cork ('corked'); reduction: 'stinky', rotten eggs, boiled cabbage/onions, blocked drains — low levels can add complexity, may dissipate once opened; sulfur dioxide: high levels acrid burnt match, lower levels mask fruit, too little → oxidation, highest in sweet whites; oxidation: closure failure, deeper browner colour, toffee, honey, caramel, coffee, lacks freshness — not a fault in deliberately oxidative styles; out of condition: lost vibrancy, dull, stale (too old or bad storage: hot, bright, variable); volatile acidity: some is normal and adds fragrance, high levels vinegar, nail polish remover; Brett: plasters, hot vinyl, smoked meat, leather, sweaty horses — some enjoy low levels
P4: Come si stabiliscono intensità e sviluppo (youthful, developing, fully developed, tired) con esempi? || Comment déterminer intensité et évolution (jeune, en évolution, pleinement évolué, fatigué) avec exemples ? || How are intensity and development (youthful, developing, fully developed, tired) determined, with examples?
K: intensity: pronounced if obvious without sniffing; light if faint after sniffing; otherwise medium(-)/medium/medium(+); development: youthful = dominated by primary/secondary (oak may stand apart, not integrated); developing = mostly primary/secondary but some tertiary (e.g. Rioja Reserva on release); fully developed = tertiary predominate (age-indicated Tawny, all Sherries on release; Vintage Port can take decades); tired/past its best = attractive aromas fade, unpleasant develop; many wines (almost all rosés, most inexpensive whites, many inexpensive reds) go quickly from youthful to tired

Q: L'aspetto e il metodo di degustazione. || L'aspect et la méthode de dégustation. || Appearance and tasting method.
P5: Come si prepara una degustazione (ambiente, persona, bicchiere, campione)? || Comment préparer une dégustation (lieu, dégustateur, verre, échantillon) ? || How do you prepare for tasting (environment, yourself, glass, sample)?
K: good natural light, odour-free, space, spittoons; clean palate (no toothpaste, strong food); well hydrated (dehydration dries nasal receptors, less saliva); notes; glass odourless, colourless, no detergent residues; ISO glass: rounded bowl for swirling, inward-sloping walls to capture aromas; consistent pour, about 5 cl
P5: Come si valutano limpidezza, intensità di colore e «altre osservazioni» (lacrime, deposito, pétillance, bollicine)? || Comment évaluer limpidité, intensité de couleur et « autres observations » (larmes, dépôt, perlant, bulles) ? || How are clarity, colour intensity and 'other observations' (legs, deposit, pétillance, bubbles) assessed?
K: clarity clear–hazy (haze = suspended particles, may indicate fault, confirm on nose/palate); intensity: glass at 45°, how far colour extends from core to rim; reds also look down at the stem (visible = pale, invisible = deep); whites with broad watery rim pale, pigment almost to rim deep; legs/tears thicker with sugar or high alcohol, pigmented in deep reds; deposit may mean unfined/unfiltered; slight spritz may be fault (refermentation, MLF in bottle) or deliberate freshness in light unoaked whites; bubbles say nothing reliable about quality (glass cleanliness)
