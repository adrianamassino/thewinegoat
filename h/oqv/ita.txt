Q: Piemonte. || Piémont. || Piemonte.
P6: Spiega perché il Nebbiolo richiede i siti migliori e descrivi Barolo e Barbaresco. || Expliquez pourquoi le nebbiolo exige les meilleurs sites et décrivez Barolo et Barbaresco. || Explain why Nebbiolo needs the best sites and describe Barolo and Barbaresco.
K: Piemonte moderate continental, long cold winters, summer storms, hail, fog; mountains to north give rain shadow; Po and Lake Maggiore moderate; foothills 150–600 m with aspects and altitudes; Barolo: horseshoe valley, villages with steep south-facing slopes 300–500 m; 100% Nebbiolo: high acid and tannin, little colour; ripens slowly → perfumed sour cherry, herbs, dried flowers; best years full, high acid and tannin, age (truffle, tar, leather); village names (Barolo Serralunga d'Alba) and crus (Barolo Cannubi) on labels; three years before release, 18 months in oak; traditionally years in large casks to soften harsh tannins; now better vineyard management and gentler extraction → riper tannins, shorter ageing, small barrels with some new oak or larger casks; Barbaresco: smaller, lower south-facing slopes 200–400 m plus river influence → ripens earlier, fruitier, less perfumed, similar acid/tannin, ages; two years, nine months oak; fewer village names, best from named crus; no IGT in Piemonte — all DOC/DOCG
P4: Confronta Barbera e Dolcetto e indica dove si coltivano. || Comparez barbera et dolcetto et indiquez où on les cultive. || Compare Barbera and Dolcetto and say where they are grown.
K: around Asti and Alba, DOCs named grape + town; Barbera most widely planted black: late ripening, medium-deep colour, low-medium tannin, high acidity, red cherry, plum, sometimes black pepper; Barbera d'Alba DOC and Barbera d'Asti DOCG (often higher quality); two styles: youthful fruity unoaked or barrel-aged spicy; Dolcetto earlier ripening than Nebbiolo and Barbera → coolest sites; Dolcetto d'Alba DOC many finest; deep often purple colour, medium-high tannin, medium acidity, black plum, red cherry, dried herbs; both can be drunk young, best age several years; in Barolo zone labelled Alba DOC; Gavi: Cortese in hills with altitude and sea breezes, long slow ripening, high acid, floral, pale, light, citrus, green apple, pear, protective steel or old oak and lees; Gavi di Gavi from town of Gavi; Asti sparkling Moscato

Q: Veneto e Nord-Est. || Vénétie et Nord-Est. || Veneto and North-East.
P6: Come si producono Amarone e Recioto della Valpolicella, e che cos'è il Ripasso? || Comment produit-on l'amarone et le recioto della Valpolicella, et qu'est-ce que le ripasso ? || How are Amarone and Recioto della Valpolicella made, and what is Ripasso?
K: Valpolicella north-west of Verona: foothills (limestone, clay, volcanic) slow ripening, more acidity; flatter south gravel and sand warmer, fruitier; Corvina: thin skin, moderate colour, low-medium tannin, high acid; local grapes added for colour/tannin; Valpolicella DOC/Classico (foothills): simple, fruity, light tannin, red cherry, rarely oaked, drink immediately; passito: grapes picked early (high acid), dried indoors in shallow crates (free of rot), concentrating sugar and flavour (and colour), fermentation in winter; Amarone DOCG dry or off-dry, full, high alcohol, medium-high tannin, concentrated red berry and spice, large oak casks; Recioto della Valpolicella DOCG so sweet fermentation stops naturally: sweet, intense red fruit, high alcohol, full, medium-high tannin; Recioto di Soave sweet white; Ripasso: unpressed skins from Amarone (drained shortly before end of fermentation) added to finished Valpolicella, yeast ferment remaining sugar, more colour, flavour, tannin; medium-full, medium-high tannin, stewed red cherry, plum
P4: Confronta Soave, Pinot Grigio delle Venezie e i bianchi del Friuli/Alto Adige. || Comparez Soave, pinot grigio delle Venezie et blancs du Frioul/Haut-Adige. || Compare Soave, Pinot Grigio delle Venezie and whites from Friuli/Alto Adige.
K: Northern Italy moderate, dry short summers, Alps shelter, lakes/rivers moderate; pergola traditionally, now VSP higher density; Soave (east of Verona): Garganega; foothills limestone, clay, volcanic, cool soils + altitude → full flavour, high acid, can age (Soave Classico); plain sandy alluvial → fruitier, medium acid, drink young; medium-high acid, medium body, pear, red apple, stone fruit, white pepper, no new oak, almond and honey with age; Veneto plain: fertile, high yields, Pinot Grigio, Chardonnay, Merlot, Garganega, Trebbiano → simple fruity Veneto IGT; fog and moisture → more sprays; Alto Adige (Bolzano, terraces, altitude, big diurnal range): Pinot Grigio dry, light-medium, high acid, citrus, green fruit; Gewurztraminer, Chardonnay, Pinot Blanc; Schiava light reds; Trentino slightly warmer, Chardonnay and Pinot Grigio, Teroldego; Friuli: richest Pinot Grigio (medium-full, peach, tropical), Friulano (pear, red apple, herbs), Merlot; Friuli Grave plain simple; Collio and Colli Orientali hills premium concentrated whites

Q: Toscana. || Toscane. || Tuscany.
P6: Descrivi il Sangiovese e confronta Chianti Classico, Brunello di Montalcino e Vino Nobile di Montepulciano. || Décrivez le sangiovese et comparez Chianti Classico, Brunello di Montalcino et Vino Nobile di Montepulciano. || Describe Sangiovese and compare Chianti Classico, Brunello di Montalcino and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano.
K: Apennines dominate central Italy; altitude moderates heat, coasts sea breezes; Sangiovese late ripening (needs warmth), high acidity and tannin, red cherry, plum, dried herbs; usually oak-aged (small or large) to soften tannins, spice; meaty, gamey with bottle age; Chianti DOCG wide area between Pisa, Florence, Siena, seven sub-zones (Rufina, Colli Senesi well known), blends with small amounts of other grapes; Chianti Classico separate DOCG, higher altitude → slower ripening, more acidity and herbal; 12 months before release, Riserva 24 months (3 in bottle), Gran Selezione single estate + 6 months more than Riserva; no oak rules but most oaked; southern Tuscany lower altitude, warmer, south-west maritime breezes → more intense, fuller; Brunello di Montalcino 100% Sangiovese, five years before sale, two in oak; Vino Nobile di Montepulciano Sangiovese with other permitted grapes, two years; declassify to Rosso di Montalcino / Rosso di Montepulciano DOC (poor vintage, young vines)
P4: Che cosa sono i «Super Tuscans» e il ruolo di Bolgheri? || Que sont les « super toscans » et quel est le rôle de Bolgheri ? || What are 'Super Tuscans', and what is Bolgheri's role?
K: flat coastal Tuscany cooled by sea breezes rather than altitude; black Bordeaux and other international varieties widely planted; first Super-Tuscan from Cabernet Sauvignon on the coast, sold at premium price though not eligible for PDO status; turning point re-establishing Italy's reputation for excellence; many producers followed, international varieties sometimes with Italian ones; many bottled as Toscana IGT; recent Bolgheri DOC and Maremma Toscana DOC permit wines entirely from non-Italian varieties (Cabernet, Merlot, Syrah)

P6: Descrivi Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, Verdicchio, Aglianico (Taurasi), Fiano e Greco. || Décrivez montepulciano d'Abruzzo, verdicchio, aglianico (Taurasi), fiano et greco. || Describe Montepulciano d'Abruzzo, Verdicchio, Aglianico (Taurasi), Fiano and Greco.
K: Montepulciano grape (no relation to Tuscan town): high colour and tannin, medium acid, black plum and cherry; many simple fruity unoaked, concentrated ones short oak; Marche reds Montepulciano + Sangiovese (Conero DOCG); Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi: naturally high acid, green apple, lemon, fennel, almond; best concentrated, honey and almond with age; South hot, dry inland, humid near coast; Apennine altitude, sea breezes in Puglia; bush vines traditionally, now cordon/trellis; Campania most DOCGs; Fiano di Avellino: medium acid, medium-full, stone fruit, melon, mango; best oaked, age to wax, honey; Greco di Tufo: more acid, leaner, green apple, stone fruit, passion fruit; steel or old oak and lees; age to honey, mushroom; Aglianico most prestigious black: deep colour, high acid and tannin, black fruit, oak-matured, ages to earth, forest floor; best in Taurasi DOCG; Aglianico del Vulture (Basilicata, extinct volcano, up to 900 m)
P4: Che vini fanno Puglia e Sicilia (Primitivo, Negroamaro, Nero d'Avola, Etna)? || Quels vins font les Pouilles et la Sicile (primitivo, negroamaro, nero d'Avola, Etna) ? || What wines do Puglia and Sicily make (Primitivo, Negroamaro, Nero d'Avola, Etna)?
K: Puglia vast agricultural, hot, black grapes; high yields → simple fruity Puglia IGT; controlled yields: Negroamaro full, medium tannin and acid, high alcohol, baked red/black fruit (Salice Salentino DOC); Primitivo (Zinfandel) similar, very ripe berry; Sicily: vast volumes, Terre Siciliane IGT or Sicilia DOC (slightly lower yields); Nero d'Avola dominant: medium-full, medium acid and tannin, plum, black cherry, fruity early or concentrated; local whites unoaked citrus, stone fruit; Chardonnay and Syrah (successful); Etna DOC: old low-yielding vines at altitude; Nerello Mascalese + Nerello Cappuccio: fragrant, high acid and tannin, sour red cherry, cranberry, raspberry, dried herbs; tannins soften, mushroom with age; whites medium body, high acid, citrus, stony/steely, honey and wax with age

Q: Il sistema legislativo italiano. || Le système législatif italien. || The Italian legal system.
P4: Spiega DOCG, DOC, IGT e i termini Classico, Superiore e Riserva. || Expliquez DOCG, DOC, IGT et les termes Classico, Superiore et Riserva. || Explain DOCG, DOC, IGT and the terms Classico, Superiore and Riserva.
K: PDO = DOP but traditional terms more common: DOC (geographical boundaries, limits on varieties and production methods), DOCG (all DOC requirements + bottled in production area + Ministry of Agriculture tasting); PGI = IGP but IGT commonly used (e.g. Toscana IGT, Veneto IGT, Puglia IGT, Terre Siciliane); Classico: original classified zone before boundaries expanded, includes many of the best (Chianti Classico separate DOCG, Soave/Valpolicella Classico = foothills); Riserva: higher alcohol and longer ageing than appellation minimums (e.g. Chianti Classico Riserva 24 months); Superiore is not defined in the textbook (credit only if consistent)

P5: Descrivi Alto Adige, Trentino e Friuli-Venezia Giulia e le loro uve principali. || Décrivez Haut-Adige, Trentin et Frioul-Vénétie Julienne et leurs principaux cépages. || Describe Alto Adige, Trentino and Friuli-Venezia Giulia and their main grapes.
K: Alto Adige: most northerly, around Bolzano, terraces on SE and SW valley sides of Adige, short dry summers, low growing-season rain, large diurnal range from altitude → aromatic whites, elegant light reds; Pinot Grigio dry, light-medium, high acid, citrus, green fruit; Gewurztraminer, Chardonnay, Pinot Blanc; Schiava light fruity raspberry, plum; mostly Alto Adige DOC; Trentino: wider valley, valley floor and slopes, slightly warmer (more southerly, lower altitudes, mountains block Lake Garda cooling); Chardonnay, Pinot Grigio (valley floor medium-bodied ripe stone fruit), Merlot, Teroldego (deep, medium-high tannin, high acid, black fruit, oak, ages); Trentino DOC; Friuli: north Alpine foothills moderate continental, south plain warm maritime; Pinot Grigio richest in Italy, Merlot most planted black, Friulano; Friuli Grave plain simple; Collio and Colli Orientali premium whites (Colli Orientali also reds)
