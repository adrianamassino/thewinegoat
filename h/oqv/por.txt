Q: Portogallo: vini fermi. || Portugal : vins tranquilles. || Portugal: still wines.
P6: Descrivi Vinho Verde e Douro (vini fermi) in clima, uve e stili. || Décrivez Vinho Verde et Douro (vins tranquilles) : climat, cépages et styles. || Describe Vinho Verde and the Douro (still wines): climate, grapes and styles.
K: Vinho Verde: north-west, near Atlantic, moderate maritime; high annual rainfall → excessive canopy growth and disease without careful canopy management; move from traditional pergolas to spur-pruned VSP for airflow, grape health and quality, mechanisation; classic white: pale lemon, high acidity, low alcohol 8–11.5% (lowest alcohol usually off-dry); up to 14% if label names grape, sub-region or authorised quality term; Loureiro, Arinto; traditionally slight sparkle enhancing freshness; Vinho Verde Alvarinho entirely Alvarinho (Albariño) from Monção e Melgaço sub-region, 11.5–14%, riper, more tropical; reds deeply coloured and tannic, rosés fresh fruity; Douro: oldest demarcated region, ~80 km east of Porto, Marão Mountains to Spanish frontier; dominated by Port but unfortified wines always made; same five varieties: Touriga Nacional (highest quality, best known, low yields, intense colour and flavour, high tannin), Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz (Tempranillo), Tinta Barroca, Tinto Cão; reds deep, full, high ripe tannin, rich black fruit, best with subtle toasty new oak; whites esp. from high altitude fresh varietal fruit, medium-high acidity
P4: Che cosa caratterizzano Dão, Bairrada e Alentejo? || Qu'est-ce qui caractérise Dão, Bairrada et Alentejo ? || What characterises Dão, Bairrada and Alentejo?
K: Dão: mountainous area 80 km south of Douro, vines on gentler hills/slopes 200–400 m; cold wet winters, warm dry summers, significant diurnal range → high-quality grapes; reds delicate red fruit, soft tannins, high acidity; new plantings Touriga Nacional, Tinta Roriz, Jaen (Mencía), Alfrocheiro (deep colour, intense blackberry, strawberry); best whites Encruzado, light fresh to richer barrel-fermented; Bairrada: between Dão and Atlantic, maritime, rainy winters, warm summers, harvest rain problem for late ripeners; Baga dominant: late ripening, small thick-skinned berries, deep colour, high tannin; underripe → high acid, astringent tannins; later picking softer, rich black fruit; gentler crushing/maceration and blending soften; also Touriga Nacional, Alfrocheiro, Cabernet, Merlot, Syrah; whites Bical and Maria Gomes; Alentejo: eight sub-regions in south-east, warm growing season; cooler wetter north more elegant, hotter drier centre/south richer; red blend Aragonez (Tempranillo) + Trincadeira (drought-tolerant, spicy red berry, high tannin), Alicante Bouschet (colour, tannin), increasing Touriga Nacional and Syrah; deep colour, high yet soft tannins, full body, expressive ripe fruit; whites Arinto, Antão Vaz, Roupeiro: fruity, floral, medium-high acid, some oak

P5: Quali uve autoctone portoghesi conosci e quali stili danno (Touriga Nacional, Baga, Alvarinho, Arinto)? || Quels cépages autochtones portugais connaissez-vous et quels styles donnent-ils (touriga nacional, baga, alvarinho, arinto) ? || Which Portuguese native grapes do you know and what styles do they give (Touriga Nacional, Baga, Alvarinho, Arinto)?
K: wealth of indigenous varieties = point of difference, but hard to understand as same grape has different names in different regions; Touriga Nacional: highest-quality Douro variety, low yields, intense colour and flavour, high tannin; also in Dão, Bairrada, Alentejo; Baga: Bairrada, late ripening, small thick-skinned berries, deep colour, high tannin, high acid/astringent if underripe; Alvarinho: Monção e Melgaço, 11.5–14%, riper tropical aromas; Arinto: Vinho Verde and Alentejo whites, fruity, floral, medium-high acid; Tinta Roriz/Aragonez = Tempranillo; Jaen = Mencía; Loureiro (Vinho Verde); Encruzado (Dão); Trincadeira, Alfrocheiro; reds supple fruity or full and tannic, whites usually refreshing, best young
P5: Spiega DOC e Vinho Regional in Portogallo. || Expliquez DOC et Vinho Regional au Portugal. || Explain DOC and Vinho Regional in Portugal.
K: PDO = DOP, traditional term DOC widely used; PGI = IGP, traditional term Vinho Regional still on labels; no GI = Vinho (success with simple fruity off-dry rosés); climate: Atlantic most important, much maritime, inland hot dry continental, altitude mitigates; temperature differences → diversity of styles; five main DOCs Vinho Verde, Douro, Dão, Bairrada, Alentejo; largest Vinho Regional by production Alentejano, based around Alentejo DOC but far larger; more liberal rules, wider range of grapes esp. international; Lisboa (north of Lisbon), Península de Setúbal (south), Tejo (north-east) important Vinho Regional areas
