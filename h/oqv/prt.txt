P6: Spiega come la fermentazione breve del Porto influenza le tecniche di estrazione e la fortificazione. || Expliquez comment la courte fermentation du Porto influence les techniques d'extraction et le mutage. || Explain how Port's short fermentation affects extraction techniques and fortification.
K: fermentation stopped by fortification at 5–9% abv → sweet; lasts only ~24–36 hours; normal red extraction not vigorous enough for expected colour and tannin; foot treading: traditional benchmark, teams tread 3–4 hours in shallow granite lagares, then regular punch-downs once fermentation under way; labour intensive, some premium; autovinifiers: sealed vats, CO2 pressure pushes juice up pipes to holding tank, valve releases, wine floods over cap; cycle every 15–20 minutes when active → much more skin contact than pumping over; limited pre-fermentation extraction, newer ones add paddles; piston plungers (round shallow open steel vats with robotic pistons) and robotic lagares (rectangular steel tank, gantry with robotic 'feet' squashing grapes, then punch-downs) imitate treading, considered as good, widely used for premium; fortification kills yeast → stable sweet wine 19–22% abv; timing depends on must weight and desired sweetness (house style); aguardente no stronger than 77% abv → about 20% of bottle volume is spirit (Fino ~3.5%); thick-skinned, high-tannin varieties, black fruit, floral: Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz, Tinta Barroca, Touriga Nacional, Tinto Cão
P4: Perché i vigneti del Douro sono difficili da coltivare e come si sono adattati (socalcos, patamares, vinha ao alto)? || Pourquoi les vignes du Douro sont-elles difficiles à cultiver et comment s'adaptent-elles (socalcos, patamares, vinha ao alto) ? || Why are Douro vineyards hard to cultivate, and how have they adapted (socalcos, patamares, vinha ao alto)?
K: vineyards start 70 km upstream of Porto/Vila Nova de Gaia; three sub-regions: Baixo Corgo (west, coolest, wettest, lightest wines), Cima Corgo (most top vineyards), Douro Superior (sparsely planted, hotter, drier); warm continental, shielded from Atlantic rain by Serra do Marão; spring frost, downpours disrupt flowering and harvest; very hot summers, very low growing-season rain; schist bedrock fractures vertically → roots reach deep water reserves; very tall steep slopes along Douro and tributaries; varied topography: altitude differences top/bottom, northerly aspects to avoid full sun; steep slopes enormous costly challenge; socalcos: narrow terraces with stone walls, few rows, no mechanisation, expensive walls, still widely used; patamares: no retaining walls, wide enough for tractors; vinha ao alto: vines up and down gentler slopes, roads across slope, machinery by winches, not viable on steepest; both newer systems prone to erosion (especially vinha ao alto)

P6: Confronta gli stili ruby (Ruby, Reserve, LBV, Vintage) e tawny (Tawny, Tawny con indicazione d'età). || Comparez les styles ruby (Ruby, Reserve, LBV, Vintage) et tawny (Tawny, Tawny avec indication d'âge). || Compare ruby styles (Ruby, Reserve, LBV, Vintage) and tawny styles (Tawny, age-indicated Tawny).
K: old oak vessels (small and very large) or steel; no new oak flavour wanted; style depends on vessel; ruby styles: minimise oxygen, short ageing in very large oak or steel; deep colour, intense primary fruit; Ruby 1–3 years, lacks concentration/complexity; Reserve: higher quality, official tasting panel (Reserve Tawny min six years in wood); LBV: single vintage, 4–6 years before bottling (large oak) → approachable; most fined and filtered, ready to drink, like premium Reserve Ruby; few unfiltered, like Vintage, can age, sediment, decant; Vintage: register intention in second year after harvest, bottle by third year; large oak or steel; unfined, unfiltered; most concentrated, tannic; ages decades, heavy sediment; with age garnet, tertiary cooked fruit, prune, leather; declared on average three times a decade, producer's choice; flagship blend of best vineyards; Single Quinta Vintage from one estate (non-declared years for large houses); tawny: long oxidative maturation in pipes; garnet then tawny, only oldest brown; primary fruit fades to raisin, walnut, coffee, chocolate, caramel; deposit thrown in wood; fully developed on release; age-indicated 10/20/30/40 years: average age, must match character, bottling year stated (lose freshness after bottling), finest Tawnies; basic Tawny no older than Ruby: lighter Baixo Corgo wines, White Port blending, hot Douro maturation, heavy fining
P4: Confronta un Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise e un Rutherglen Muscat. || Comparez un muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise et un muscat de Rutherglen. || Compare a Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise and a Rutherglen Muscat.
K: Muscat varieties: low-medium acidity, perfumed orange blossom, rose, grape; thrive in warm/hot but best sites have cooling influence to keep acid and aromas; two styles; youthful unaged (e.g. Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise, Southern Rhône): medium gold, floral, aromatic, pure varietal character, sweet but rarely luscious; ripe healthy grapes, no raisining/drying (would mask varietal); some skin contact (also during fermentation) for aromatic intensity and richness; cool fermentation stopped with 96% grape spirit; stored in inert vessels, protected from oxygen; fully developed aged (e.g. Rutherglen, Australia): amber to brown, sweet or luscious; style set by harvest timing, fortification timing, length/type of ageing; some raisining before picking for luscious style (too much loses varietal aromas); fermentation nearly always on skins; early fortification for luscious; oxidative ageing for decades in old wood (large vessels), new oak would clash; sometimes warm conditions; retain Muscat aromas; oldest given aromatic lift by blending in younger wine

P4: Dove matura il Porto e perché questo influisce sullo stile? || Où vieillit le Porto et pourquoi cela influe-t-il sur le style ? || Where does Port mature, and why does this affect style?
K: industry split between Porto/Vila Nova de Gaia (twin cities at Douro mouth) and vineyard area 70 km upstream; wines generally transported downstream to Vila Nova de Gaia: cooler coastal climate suits slow maturation; some (notably for Tawny) always stored up the Douro: higher temperatures → faster ageing and loss of colour; air-conditioned warehouses → more ageing in vineyard area; most Ports complex blends of varieties and sites, blending ongoing during pre-bottle maturation; all non-vintage except LBV and Vintage (single vintage); vast majority red, small white and rosé
