Q: Prezzo e qualità del vino. || Prix et qualité du vin. || Wine price and quality.
P6: Quali costi di vigneto e di cantina spiegano la differenza di prezzo tra un vino economico e uno premium? || Quels coûts au vignoble et à la cave expliquent l'écart de prix entre un vin bon marché et un haut de gamme ? || Which vineyard and winery costs explain the price difference between an inexpensive and a premium wine?
K: establishing a vineyard expensive: land (premium regions), soil preparation, vines, trellis, years before return; labour usually largest cost, reduced by seasonal workforce, mechanisation, sharing; isolated or very steep sites (Mosel) much higher costs; winery equipment, high-tech machines, sharing (bottling); maturation time before release (sometimes required by law): storage costs and capital tied up in stock, delay in profit; packaging (bottles, labels, capsules, closures, cartons; unusual/heavy bottles cost more; bulk shipping and bottling in market only for large producers and not always permitted); transport, distribution and sales via partners; taxes (vary by country, control consumption and raise revenue); retailer margins (restaurants more than shops: occasion, setting, service); market forces: price set by what market will pay, high demand + limited supply (Bordeaux estates) raises price beyond cost; lack of demand → Mosel steep sites abandoned
P4: Che cosa sono le GI europee (DOP/IGP) e quali regole stabiliscono le denominazioni? || Que sont les IG européennes (AOP/IGP) et quelles règles fixent les appellations ? || What are EU GIs (PDO/PGI) and what rules do appellations set?
K: GI = designated vineyard area, from region (Bordeaux) to single vineyard (La Romanée); WTO system of boundaries and names, mutual recognition (Australia stopped using 'Chablis'); usually 85% from stated GI (similar for vintage and variety), PDO 100%; EU PDO: smaller, tighter rules, specify permitted varieties, growing and winemaking techniques to protect identity, quality, prevent fraud; often traditional terms instead (AC rather than AOP); variety often not stated (Chablis, Meursault); PGI (IGP increasingly replaces Vin de Pays): larger, fewer rules, non-traditional varieties (Sauvignon in southern France), still yield limits, very diverse quality, varieties usually labelled; wine without GI: most flexible, multi-regional or multinational EU blends; non-EU: areas only, no limits on varieties planted, legal terms (AVA, Zone, Ward) rarely on labels

Q: Leggi sul vino ed etichetta. || Lois sur le vin et étiquetage. || Wine law and labelling.
P5: Quali termini d'etichetta regolati per legge (es. Riserva, Gran Reserva, Prädikat) e non regolati (es. Old Vines, Reserve fuori UE) conosci, e perché la differenza conta? || Quels termes d'étiquette réglementés (ex. Riserva, Gran Reserva, Prädikat) et non réglementés (ex. Old Vines, Reserve hors UE) connaissez-vous, et pourquoi la différence compte-t-elle ? || Which regulated labelling terms (e.g. Riserva, Gran Reserva, Prädikat) and unregulated ones (e.g. Old Vines, Reserve outside the EU) do you know, and why does the difference matter?
K: EU has legally defined quality and style indications observed by all EU countries and protected abroad (Australia can no longer use Fino, Amontillado); examples from country chapters: Spanish Crianza/Reserva/Gran Reserva ageing, Italian Classico/Riserva/Superiore, German Prädikat by must weight, Burgundy Premier/Grand Cru; label integrity: seller responsible for accurate, checkable claims; GI, vintage, variety percentage rules (typically 85%); unregulated or loosely defined terms: 'old vines' (no legal meaning), Chilean Reserva/Gran Reserva loosely defined, carry little meaning but show levels in a producer's range; regulated terms give minimum guarantees, unregulated depend on producer
P5: Quali sono gli obiettivi della legislazione sul vino in materia di sicurezza alimentare e consumo responsabile? || Quels sont les objectifs de la législation viticole en matière de sécurité alimentaire et de consommation responsable ? || What are the aims of wine legislation regarding food safety and responsible consumption?
K: three issues: safe for consumption, accurate labelling, reducing harm; processing agents and chemicals regulated but rarely on labels; SO2 tightly regulated (toxic at high doses), 'contains sulfites' above a level, even without additions since fermentation produces some; alcohol not harmful in moderation but excess affects behaviour, addictive, health; minimum legal age for purchase/consumption (young people vulnerable); blood alcohol limits for driving (coordination, reaction, risk-taking); sensible drinking guidelines in units per day/week for men and women; limits on advertising and marketing (voluntary codes or law, lifestyle claims, responsible drinking messages)

Q: Tipi di produttori. || Types de producteurs. || Types of producer.
P6: Confronta cooperative, négociant (merchant) ed estate (domaine): funzionamento, vantaggi e limiti. || Comparez coopératives, négociants et domaines : fonctionnement, avantages et limites. || Compare co-operatives, merchants (négociants) and estates (domaines): how they work, advantages and limits.
K: terms have no legal significance, rarely on labels; one producer may sell estate and merchant wines; co-operatives: owned by members (growers) who deliver grapes to co-op winery run by employed winemakers; growers guaranteed a buyer; winemaker may lack control over grape quality; not always inferior, sometimes best in area; very common in Europe because holdings are too small to make and market own wine; merchants: buy grapes, juice or wine from growers or co-ops under contract, more control in theory, can reject grapes, but supply hard in high demand regions; small premium or large multi-level; common outside Europe where growers have larger holdings; many big brands; négociant; estates: only own grapes, small scale, greatest control, usually best wines, but fewer blending options in poor years (frost, hail); domaine
