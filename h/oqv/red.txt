Q: Vinificazione dei vini rossi. || Vinification des vins rouges. || Red winemaking.
P6: Quali tecniche di estrazione esistono durante la fermentazione e come le si sceglie in base allo stile? || Quelles techniques d'extraction existent pendant la fermentation et comment les choisir selon le style ? || What extraction techniques exist during fermentation, and how are they chosen for style?
K: skins in the vessel to extract colour and tannin; pressing after fermentation; MLF standard; pre-fermentation cold maceration/cold soak extracts colour and flavour (tannin more soluble in alcohol, so little extracted); fermentation 20–32°C (higher than whites) aids extraction, not above 35°C (kills yeast); lowering temperature near the end reduces tannin extraction; cap of skins: if left floating little extraction; punching down (very effective, risk of over-extraction late → bitter, astringent), pumping over (wets cap, dissipates heat, oxygenates), rack and return (very extractive, once or twice, dissipates heat), rotary fermenters (constant contact); duration and frequency control extraction; large often open-top vessels (oak, concrete, steel), not barrels; post-fermentation maceration extracts more tannin, long periods may smooth tannins; light fruity → less extraction; structured → more
P6: Spiega macerazione carbonica e fermentazione a grappolo intero, la pressatura (vin de goutte/vin de presse) e la produzione di rosati. || Expliquez macération carbonique et fermentation en grappes entières, le pressurage (vin de goutte/vin de presse) et la production de rosés. || Explain carbonic maceration and whole-bunch fermentation, pressing (free-run/press wine) and rosé production.
K: whole bunches need ripe stems (else bitter); oxygen-free → intracellular fermentation (alcohol in berries without yeast) and distinctive fruity aromas; carbonic maceration: only whole bunches, vat filled with CO2, skins split at ~2% alcohol, pressed, yeast finish off skins; colour but little tannin, soft fruity, kirsch, banana, bubble gum, cinnamon; semi-carbonic: no added CO2, bottom grapes crushed and ferment, CO2 fills vat; some premium Pinot continue on skins with punch downs → better integration, fresher fruit; whole bunches with crushed fruit → silkier texture, brighter fruit; free run drawn off, rest pressed: press wine deeper colour, more tannin as pressing continues; press fractions used to adjust blend; rosé: direct pressing (palest), short maceration then drained and fermented cool like white, blending red into white (not allowed in EU except rosé Champagne; some New World inexpensive); oak rarely used for rosé

Q: Maturazione dei vini rossi. || Élevage des vins rouges. || Red wine maturation.
P6: Confronta maturazione in barrique nuove, botti grandi vecchie e recipienti inerti, con esempi di vini. || Comparez l'élevage en barriques neuves, en grands foudres anciens et en cuves inertes, avec des exemples. || Compare maturation in new barriques, large old casks and inert vessels, with examples.
K: whether to use oak is the key maturation decision; reds benefit less from lees contact; inert stainless steel/concrete keep pure fruit (inexpensive reds); oak barrels or chips/staves; inexpensive: months in second/third-fill barrels or chips for toast, smooth harsh tannins; premium: trend away from 100% small new oak towards subtlety and integration with vessels of different ages and larger sizes; best wines longer and more new oak (need concentrated fruit); Cabernet: French oak barriques, high proportion new, 6 months to 3–4 years, 12–18 typical; Merlot 12–18 months, new oak toast, vanilla, clove suits plum fruit; Pinot 12–24 months, mostly second/third-fill (new oak overpowers delicate fruit); restrained Syrah 300–500 L older barrels; Grenache large old foudres (new oak overwhelms); blending oaked with unoaked wine for subtler oak, blending barrels of different ages/sizes/toast for complexity; oxygen softens tannin, tertiary notes

Q: Rossi premium per varietà. || Rouges haut de gamme par cépage. || Premium reds by variety.
P6: Spiega come si vinificano Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot e Pinot Noir premium e perché le scelte differiscono. || Expliquez la vinification des cabernet-sauvignon, merlot et pinot noir haut de gamme et pourquoi les choix diffèrent. || Explain how premium Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Pinot Noir are made and why the choices differ.
K: Cabernet: thick skins, abundant colour, flavour, tannin, late ripening (astringent, herbaceous if unripe); destemmed (stems herbaceous, astringent) and crushed, pre-fermentation maceration for colour; ferment 26–30°C; avoid high temperatures and extractive cap work late in fermentation; post-fermentation maceration for gentler extraction, softer tannins; high new oak proportion; blended with Merlot (softness, fruit, body) or Shiraz in Australia; Haut-Médoc: grippy young tannins, cedar, blackcurrant leaf; New World ripe blackcurrant, black cherry, smooth (Napa, Coonawarra, Margaret River, Hawke's Bay, Stellenbosch, Colchagua); Merlot: buds and ripens earlier, more supple skins → less rigorous extraction; international late-harvest style (purple, blackberry, plum, velvety, new oak) vs earlier Bordeaux style (medium body, higher acid, red fruit, leafy); Pinot: early budding/ripening, thin skin, many clones; colour and tannin tricky, maximise extraction without overworking; pre-fermentation maceration; whole bunches increasingly (red fruit, floral); ferment often above 30°C (cooler for lighter styles); little post-fermentation maceration; not blended; too hot climate → jammy
P4: Come si vinificano Syrah/Shiraz e Grenache in stile pieno o più sobrio? || Comment vinifie-t-on syrah/shiraz et grenache en style puissant ou plus retenu ? || How are Syrah/Shiraz and Grenache made in a full or more restrained style?
K: Syrah: small thick dark skins, won't ripen in very cool climates; medium-bodied pepper and black fruit to full, ripe, liquorice; full style: warm/hot, ripe or over-ripe grapes, vigorous cap management, high new oak; restrained: earlier harvest (lower alcohol), gentler cap management, some whole bunches, extended post-fermentation maceration for fine tannins, 300–500 L older barrels; Grenache: late ripening, drought tolerant, thin-skinned sweet grapes → high alcohol, low acidity, soft tannin, red fruit; destemmed, crushed, pre-fermentation maceration, some whole bunches; open-top steel/cement or old oak; gentle punching down for finesse; usually drained at end; large old foudres; usually blended (Carignan, Mourvèdre add freshness, tannin, colour; Châteauneuf with Syrah, Mourvèdre; Priorat with Carignan; Rioja adds perfume, body, alcohol); Australia now more restrained, old bush vines

Q: Rossi economici di grande volume. || Rouges bon marché de grand volume. || High-volume inexpensive reds.
P5: Quali varietà e tecniche si usano per i rossi economici di grande volume e perché il Pinot Noir non è adatto? || Quels cépages et techniques pour les rouges bon marché et pourquoi le pinot noir ne convient-il pas ? || Which varieties and techniques are used for high-volume inexpensive reds, and why is Pinot Noir unsuitable?
K: warm sunny dry regions; Cabernet, Merlot, Syrah/Shiraz, Grenache thrive in heat and keep colour, tannin, flavour at high yields; less colour, lighter tannin, less complexity, homogeneous; Pinot hard to grow, prefers cool, little colour/tannin at high yields; fruity low-tannin style; protective handling, SO2; destem and crush; tartaric acid addition; pre-fermentation maceration not always (vat space); commercial yeast ~22–25°C for fresh fruit; static or rotary vessels; cap not heavily worked; no post-fermentation maceration (or a small part macerated and blended for structure); carbonic/semi-carbonic for Grenache and basic Beaujolais; stainless steel or months in second/third-fill barrels or chips/staves; stabilised, fined, sterile filtered, SO2 topped up; varietal or blended 'fruity red'
