Q: Rodano settentrionale e meridionale. || Rhône septentrional et méridional. || Northern and Southern Rhône.
P6: Confronta clima, suoli, uve e stili del Rodano settentrionale e meridionale. || Comparez climat, sols, cépages et styles du Rhône nord et sud. || Compare climate, soils, grapes and styles of the Northern and Southern Rhône.
K: North (Vienne to Valence): narrow steep valley, best sites steep south-facing slopes in lateral valleys sheltered from the mistral; vines on individual stakes/tepees; moderate continental like Beaujolais, cooler than south; Syrah only black grape, at northern limit → southerly steep sites; deep colour, medium-high tannin, black fruit, black pepper or floral; oak varies (new vs older/larger); co-fermentation with whites now rare (stabilises colour, Viognier adds aroma); small share of Rhône volume but many premium wines; South: valley opens out, flatter, stony soils/galets store heat; Mediterranean, mild winters, warm dry summers, drought; mistral stronger, windbreaks; Grenache bush-trained low (wind, soil warmth), Syrah trellised; complex blends (sometimes a dozen+ varieties); Grenache dominant, concentrated spiced red fruit, jammy in hot years; Syrah colour and tannin (struggles in hottest sites); Mourvèdre at northern limit, colour, very high tannin, black fruit, gamey, meaty; Cinsault red fruit, rosés; manage high tannin and alcohol; styles from light fruity to full tannic spicy, meaty, earthy
P6: Descrivi la gerarchia delle denominazioni del Rodano meridionale e cosa caratterizza Châteauneuf-du-Pape. || Décrivez la hiérarchie des appellations du Rhône sud et ce qui caractérise Châteauneuf-du-Pape. || Describe the Southern Rhône appellation hierarchy and what characterises Châteauneuf-du-Pape.
K: Côtes du Rhône: more than half of southern production, mostly medium-bodied fruity simple reds, also rosé and white; Côtes du Rhône Villages: stricter minimum alcohol, maximum yields, more Grenache/Syrah/Mourvèdre; named villages (e.g. Cairanne) 100% from that village, more body, structure, spiced red fruit, can apply for cru status; crus: Châteauneuf-du-Pape, Tavel (rosé only), Lirac (rosé, reds and whites like Châteauneuf), Gigondas and Vacqueyras (Grenache-led, full, spicy, often as good as Châteauneuf), Beaumes-de-Venise (Muscat VDN separate AC); Châteauneuf: east bank, largest cru, first AC in France; flat, galets, many soils and aspects, 13 varieties, Grenache dominates (some 100%), others more Mourvèdre; best full, richly textured, concentrated spiced red fruit balancing high alcohol; much mediocre dilute unbalanced wine; small amount of white; Tavel/Lirac rosés from Grenache and Cinsault, fuller and more intense than Provence, can age

Q: Rodano settentrionale. || Rhône septentrional. || Northern Rhône.
P6: Confronta Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph, Cornas e Condrieu. || Comparez Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph, Cornas et Condrieu. || Compare Côte-Rôtie, Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph, Cornas and Condrieu.
K: Côte-Rôtie: most northerly, around Ampuis, 'roasted slope', red only, up to 20% Viognier (rarely more than small %), deep, full, spicy, best have floral aromatic freshness and elegance; Condrieu: Viognier only, low-yield old vines on steep terraces; Château-Grillet single-property appellation; Saint-Joseph: long west bank Condrieu to Tournon, mostly Syrah reds (some Marsanne/Roussanne whites), best terraces near Tournon like Hermitage, flatter fertile valley floor/plateau light peppery, some carbonic; Hermitage: steep south-facing hill behind Tain, lieux-dits outside appellation, traditionally blended, fullest-bodied northern red, ages well, up to 15% white grapes (rare), whites about a fifth; Crozes-Hermitage: largest volume, around Hermitage, northern slopes concentrated tannic, southern flat plain higher-yielding lighter, less reputation, up to 15% Marsanne/Roussanne; Cornas: most southerly, warmest, sheltered sun-baked south-facing lateral valleys, 100% Syrah, deep full like Hermitage
P4: Descrivi i bianchi del Rodano: Viognier, Marsanne e Roussanne, e i bianchi del sud. || Décrivez les blancs du Rhône : viognier, marsanne et roussanne, et les blancs du sud. || Describe Rhône whites: Viognier, Marsanne and Roussanne, and southern whites.
K: Viognier: full body, perfumed blossom and apricot, stone fruit, low acidity, high alcohol because signature aromas develop only very late at high sugar; careful handling to avoid oily character; new oak used carefully (can overwhelm); some off-dry late harvest; Marsanne richness and weight, Roussanne acidity and perfumed fruit, usually blended, less aromatic than Viognier, age well to hazelnut; Southern Rhône whites minority: also Clairette, Grenache Blanc, Bourboulenc; richly textured, full, high alcohol, low-medium acidity, subtle fruit, new oak rarely used; no southern equivalent of Condrieu
