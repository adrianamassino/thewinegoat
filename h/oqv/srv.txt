Q: Conservazione e servizio del vino. || Conservation et service du vin. || Wine storage and service.
P4: Quali condizioni di conservazione a lungo termine raccomanda il libro e perché? || Quelles conditions de conservation longue recommande le livre et pourquoi ? || What long-term storage conditions does the book recommend and why?
K: cool and constant temperature, preferably 10–15°C (extremes of heat and cold damage); kitchen one of worst places (fluctuations); long refrigeration hardens corks, seal fails, wine goes stale, sparkling loses fizz; cork-sealed bottles on their side so cork stays in contact with wine and does not dry out letting air oxidise the wine; screwcaps can stand up; away from strong light (heats wine, ages it prematurely; artificial light can cause unpleasant flavours); away from vibration
P6: Spiega le temperature di servizio per diversi stili e come affrontare il servizio di uno spumante, un rosso con deposito e un vino difettoso. || Expliquez les températures de service selon les styles et comment servir un effervescent, un rouge à dépôt et un vin défectueux. || Explain serving temperatures for different styles and how to handle a sparkling wine, a red with sediment and a faulty wine.
K: medium/full oaked whites (white Burgundy, Fumé Blanc) lightly chilled 10–13°C; light/medium whites (Muscadet, Pinot Grigio, NZ Sauvignon, Fino) chilled 7–10°C; sweet wines (Sauternes, Eiswein) well chilled 6–8°C; sparkling (Champagne, Cava, Asti) well chilled 6–10°C; light reds (Beaujolais, Valpolicella) lightly chilled 13°C; medium/full reds room temperature 15–18°C; reds too cold thin and harsh, warm gently (not radiator); above 18°C lose freshness, muddled; over-chilling masks flavour; ice bucket 3/4 full equal ice and water; sparkling: chill (reduces pressure), remove foil, loosen cage holding cork, tilt ~30°, turn the bottle not the cork, ease out with quiet 'phut'; deposit: remove bottle horizontally (basket), open gently, pour in front of light, stop when deposit reaches neck; after opening pour a sample to check condition

Q: Bicchieri, decantazione e conservazione delle bottiglie aperte. || Verres, carafage et conservation des bouteilles ouvertes. || Glassware, decanting and preserving opened bottles.
P5: Quali bicchieri per rossi, bianchi/rosati, spumanti e fortificati, e come vanno preparati? || Quels verres pour rouges, blancs/rosés, effervescents et mutés, et comment les préparer ? || Which glasses for reds, whites/rosés, sparkling and fortified wines, and how should they be prepared?
K: reds in larger glasses: large wine surface in contact with air develops aromas and flavours; whites/rosés medium glasses gather fresh fruit aromas towards top; sparkling in flutes: bubbles travel through more wine before bursting, enhancing fizz and aroma; saucer glasses lose bubbles quickly; fortified small glasses due to high alcohol but big enough to swirl and nose; glasses must be clean: detergent or salt residues give strange flavours and make sparkling lose bubbles; polish before use with linen cloth (no fluff)
P5: Quando serve decantare, aerare un vino serve, e come si conserva una bottiglia aperta? || Quand faut-il carafer, aérer sert-il, et comment conserver une bouteille ouverte ? || When is decanting needed, does 'airing' work, and how is an opened bottle preserved?
K: decant wines with heavy deposit (natural in many good aged reds); some young wines benefit from aeration of decanting, though swirling in glass does the same; opening bottle in advance does nothing (too little wine in contact with air); opened wine loses aromatic intensity in days, then oxidises and develops vinegar; recork and fridge extends a few days; vacuum systems remove oxygen (unsuitable for sparkling, lose bubbles); blanket systems cover wine with gas heavier than oxygen; 75 cl = 6×125 ml, 4×175 ml, 3×250 ml
