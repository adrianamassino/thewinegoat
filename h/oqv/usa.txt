K: exceptionally cold California Current more crucial than latitude; cold air and fog drawn in evenings, slow to burn off → cooling into the day; Napa ~50 km long, ~5 km wide, most expensive land, Mayacamas (west) and Vaca (east) Mountains; morning fogs and afternoon breezes from San Pablo Bay moderate southern vineyards; Los Carneros most southerly, extends into Sonoma, cool → Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, still and traditional method sparkling; north along floor warm enough for Cabernet: Stags Leap, Yountville, Oakville, Rutherford (least bay influence, warmest by day, most power and structure); Merlot widely planted, Chardonnay and Sauvignon ripe tropical; St Helena and Calistoga warmest, most concentrated, full; Chalk Hill gap relief, cool nights retain acid; valley sides above fog, cooled by altitude; aspect: west-facing Howell Mountain, Atlas Peak warmer (fuller, higher alcohol) than east-facing Diamond Mountain, Spring Mountain, Mount Veeder; valley-side reds more tannin, less richness than floor; Chardonnay citrus/stone fruit, higher acidity than valley floor
P4: Che cosa caratterizza Russian River Valley, Sonoma Coast e Dry Creek Valley? || Qu'est-ce qui caractérise Russian River Valley, Sonoma Coast et Dry Creek Valley ? || What characterises Russian River Valley, Sonoma Coast and Dry Creek Valley?
K: Sonoma larger than Napa, more varied climates; Russian River Valley: Petaluma Gap → very cool, foggy; high-quality elegant Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, also sparkling; AVAs north perceptibly warmer; Dry Creek Valley: hillsides above fog → high-quality old-vine Zinfandel, valley floor suited to Sauvignon Blanc; Rhône varieties and Cabernet popular; Alexander Valley warmer still, fleshy, full; soft-textured full Cabernet; Sonoma Coast: extensive, west, greatly influenced by cold Pacific, vines often on south-facing slopes to maximise ripening; Sonoma Valley parallel to Napa, San Pablo Bay breezes/fog, Chardonnay and old-vine Zinfandel; Mendocino: Anderson Valley very cool (Riesling, Gewurztraminer, sparkling), Mendocino AVA warmer, drier behind hills up to 900 m, full, soft tannins

P4: Come si produce l'Icewine canadese e perché l'Ontario è adatto? || Comment produit-on l'icewine canadien et pourquoi l'Ontario convient-il ? || How is Canadian Icewine made, and why is Ontario suitable?
K: continental climate: low average annual temperatures but very hot July/August days; large lakes moderate extremes; cold winters far below zero for long periods → conditions for Icewine; grapes left on vine to freeze, picked and pressed frozen so water stays as ice and only concentrated sweet juice is extracted; Niagara Peninsula (south-west corner of Lake Ontario): lake cools and heats slowly → extends autumn growing season for full ripening, delays budburst limiting frost; airflow reduces fungal disease; Riesling finest Icewines (and fresh dry/off-dry); Vidal hybrid, hardy, little character dry but high-quality Icewine without Riesling's acid structure and aromatic complexity; VQA: 100% grapes from province, 100% vinifera (or permitted hybrids such as Vidal); Ontario and British Columbia; Okanagan: rain shadow, semi-desert, long days, glacial lakes

P4: Spiega il sistema delle AVA e cosa può comparire in etichetta. || Expliquez le système des AVA et ce qui peut figurer sur l'étiquette. || Explain the AVA system and what can appear on the label.
K: GI system based on American Viticultural Areas; AVAs vary greatly in size (Rutherford very small in Napa vs Central Coast from San Francisco to Santa Barbara); can cross state lines and overlap (Sta. Rita Hills within Santa Ynez Valley); federal laws set minimum percentages for single variety, vintage or AVA on label; many states have own differing laws; wines not qualifying for AVA can name state (California) or county (Napa County), with often more lenient vintage/origin rules
P6: Descrivi clima e principali stili californiani di Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir e Merlot. || Décrivez climat et principaux styles californiens de cabernet sauvignon, chardonnay, pinot noir et merlot. || Describe California's climate and main styles of Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Merlot.
K: ~1100 km long; latitude minor, cold California Current crucial; coastal mountains shield much of state; gaps let cooling inland; no ocean exposure (Central Valley) → hot; lack of ripening-season rain → drip irrigation, water shortages; scarce autumn rain → long hang time → very ripe, concentrated, very high sugar; recently more appreciation of freshness, earlier picking; Cabernet most planted black: full, very concentrated ripe cassis, spice from new oak; some fresher; Chardonnay most planted overall: Central Valley fruity, low-medium acid, staves/chips; past premium very full, high alcohol, low acid, oak, hazelnut, butter, peach, banana; now restrained less-oaked from cooler Carneros, Russian River; Pinot Noir premium from Russian River, Carneros, Santa Maria: ripe red cherry, strawberry, sweet spice, some lighter gamey; Merlot soft tannins ripe black fruit; better from cooler Monterey, Napa, Sonoma: deep, velvety, luscious, high alcohol, blackberry, plum; Sauvignon best coastal (high acid, ripe citrus), Fumé Blanc oaked; Syrah full rich
