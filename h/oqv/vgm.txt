Q: Gestione del vigneto: forme di allevamento e potatura. || Conduite du vignoble : conduite et taille. || Vineyard management: training and pruning.
P6: Confronta potatura a sperone (cordone) e a capo (Guyot), alberello e spalliera, e perché si scelgono. || Comparez taille à coursons (cordon) et à long bois (Guyot), gobelet et palissage, et pourquoi on les choisit. || Compare spur pruning (cordon) and replacement cane pruning (Guyot), bush vines and trellised vines, and why each is chosen.
K: training = shape of permanent wood: head training (little permanent wood, trunk or short arms; spur or cane pruned) vs cordon training (trunk with horizontal cordons, usually spur pruned, longer to establish, eases mechanisation); low training uses soil heat, high training avoids frost; winter pruning sets number and position of buds (spaced apart); spur = 2–3 buds; replacement cane = 8–20 buds, one or two canes tied horizontally (single/double Guyot), more complex, skilled labour; summer pruning trims canopy, directs sugar to grapes, leaf stripping exposes bunches; bush vines (untrellised, head-trained, spur-pruned): warm/hot dry sunny regions (Southern Rhône, Barossa) where shade protects grapes; in cool/wet areas shade impedes ripening and poor airflow promotes disease (Beaujolais ties shoot tips: gobelet); no machine harvesting; trellis: control sunlight (open canopy in low light, shade against sunburn), air circulation against fungal disease, mechanisation and spraying; VSP narrow open canopy; in hot regions shoot tops allowed to flop over for shade and still machine-harvest
P4: Che cosa sono vigore, gestione della chioma e densità d'impianto e come incidono su resa e qualità? || Que sont vigueur, gestion du feuillage et densité de plantation et quel est leur effet sur rendement et qualité ? || What are vigour, canopy management and planting density, and how do they affect yield and quality?
K: density 1,000–10,000 vines/ha; very limited water → low density so roots don't compete; low nutrients and enough rain → high density for root competition restricting vegetative growth (common in Europe) with strict bud numbers; too few buds → excess vigour; too many → struggle to ripen; very fertile New World soils → low density with multiple cordons or canes, good quality at high yields; yields measured in t/ha or hl/ha, predicted from bud numbers but frost, fruit set, pests reduce them; green harvesting after veraison can reduce yield but if mistimed berries swell and dilute; the book says it is very hard to make any solid link between yield and quality

Q: Rischi del vigneto, vendemmia e scelte di coltivazione. || Risques au vignoble, vendanges et choix de culture. || Vineyard hazards, harvest and growing choices.
P6: Descrivi parassiti e malattie principali (oidio, peronospora, botrytis, virus) e i mezzi di controllo. || Décrivez parasites et maladies principaux (oïdium, mildiou, botrytis, virus) et les moyens de lutte. || Describe the main pests and diseases (powdery mildew, downy mildew, botrytis, viruses) and their controls.
K: downy and powdery mildew: thrive warm humid, affect all green parts; grapes lose fruit, mouldy bitter taint; traditionally sulfur (powdery) and Bordeaux mixture copper (downy), now other chemicals, more sprays in maritime climates, stop before harvest for residues; grey rot (Botrytis cinerea): damp conditions, taints flavour, colour loss in black grapes; noble rot beneficial form for sweet whites; open canopy improves airflow, evaporation, fewer sprays; viruses: contagious, spread by cuttings or nematodes, reduce yield and quality, no cure — dig up and sanitise; bacterial diseases spread by sharpshooters, quarantine; nematodes: microscopic worms attacking roots, transmit viruses — sanitise soil, resistant rootstocks; birds (netting), mammals (fencing), half-eaten grapes raise rot risk; insects: insecticides or integrated pest management; phylloxera: rootstocks
P4: Vendemmia manuale o meccanica: vantaggi e svantaggi. || Vendange manuelle ou mécanique : avantages et inconvénients. || Hand versus machine harvesting: advantages and disadvantages.
K: harvest timing: balance of sugar, acid, flavour, tannin for desired style; may bring forward for hail/rain; machine: shakes trunk, collects berries leaving stalks; not selective, MOG (sorting at winery); fast (bad weather, Sauvignon Blanc over-ripens quickly), works at night bringing cool grapes (saves cooling energy, slows oxidation); only flat/gentle slopes, varieties not easily damaged, cannot give whole bunches (Beaujolais, Champagne); hand: slower, labour intensive, costly without workforce; selection in vineyard (essential for noble rot); less damage, shallow trays, whole bunches with stems (clean juice in pressing, whole-bunch red ferments); only option on steep slopes (Douro, Mosel, Northern Rhône); premium wine possible either way

Q: Viticoltura sostenibile, biologica e biodinamica; irrigazione. || Viticulture durable, biologique et biodynamique ; irrigation. || Sustainable, organic and biodynamic viticulture; irrigation.
P6: Confronta approcci convenzionale, sostenibile/integrato, biologico e biodinamico e spiega pro e contro dell'irrigazione a goccia e a scorrimento. || Comparez approches conventionnelle, durable/intégrée, biologique et biodynamique, et les avantages et inconvénients de l'irrigation au goutte-à-goutte et par submersion. || Compare conventional, sustainable/integrated, organic and biodynamic approaches, and the pros and cons of drip and flood irrigation.
K: conventional: regular systematic man-made chemicals and fertilisers (increased in later 20th century), environmental damage concerns; sustainable: man-made chemicals allowed but restricted, understanding pest life cycles and weather forecasts to predict and time sprays, fewer applications; integrated pest management, predators, biodiversity, plants other than vines provide habitats and nutrients; organic: only very limited traditional treatments in small quantities, certification body needed, standards vary, conversion period required; biodynamic: Steiner and Maria Thun, organic plus philosophy/cosmology, cycles of planets, moon, stars, homeopathic 'preparations', certification; irrigation where law permits: drip most advanced and expensive, computer-controlled per vine; sprinklers cheaper but waste water, damp → disease, can protect from frost; flood cheap but only flat sites with plenty of water

Q: Scelta del sito, impianto e vigne vecchie. || Choix du site, plantation et vieilles vignes. || Site selection, planting and old vines.
P5: Quali fattori ambientali, economici e varietali si valutano scegliendo un nuovo vigneto, e come si impianta? || Quels facteurs environnementaux, économiques et variétaux évalue-t-on pour un nouveau vignoble, et comment le plante-t-on ? || Which environmental, business and variety factors are assessed when choosing a new vineyard, and how is it planted?
K: environmental: average temperature, rainfall, sunlight hours, soil fertility and drainage → variety, density, training/trellising; business: utilities (power, water), workforce, machine access, land cost; variety must suit climate, have demand, EU legal restrictions; planting: clear vegetation, test soil and fertilise, pre-grafted nursery vines planted by hand or machine, plastic sleeves against animals, irrigation sometimes allowed for young vines even where otherwise banned; first crop in third year
P5: Che cosa dice il manuale sulle «vecchie vigne» e sui reimpianti, e come ragiona il viticoltore in un clima caldo e secco rispetto a uno costiero piovoso? || Que dit le manuel des « vieilles vignes » et des replantations, et comment raisonne le vigneron en climat chaud et sec face à un climat côtier pluvieux ? || What does the textbook say about 'old vines' and replanting, and how does a grower reason in a hot dry climate versus a rainy coastal one?
K: vines usually replaced at 30–50 years; 'old vines' thought to give exceptional fruit, more concentrated, but yield falls and disease susceptibility rises: balance quality and profitability; land left fallow 3+ years; replanting cycle keeps most of vineyard productive; hot, sunny, dry inland with little irrigation: low density so each vine has enough water; shade against sunburn (bush vines or VSP with flopping tops); little disease pressure; moderate rainy coastal with low fertility: high density for competition; damp → fungal disease and cloud → VSP open aerated canopy maximising light
