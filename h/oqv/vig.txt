Q: Vitis vinifera, specie americane e innesti. || Vitis vinifera, espèces américaines et greffage. || Vitis vinifera, American species and grafting.
P5: Che cos'è la fillossera e perché la maggior parte delle viti vinifera è innestata? || Qu'est-ce que le phylloxéra et pourquoi la plupart des vinifera sont-elles greffées ? || What is phylloxera, and why are most vinifera vines grafted?
K: insect native to North America, accidentally introduced to Europe in the 19th century, wholesale destruction; complex life cycle, one phase underground feeding on roots; infections enter feeding wounds, vine weakened and dies over a few years; V. vinifera cannot defend itself; American vines evolved with it: clog its mouth with sticky sap and form protective layers behind wounds; cannot be controlled with chemicals; first response planting American species/hybrids; solution by end of 19th century: graft vinifera onto American or hybrid rootstock (protection + European flavour); now in nearly every region, exceptions Chile, parts of Argentina, South Australia (strict quarantine); cuttings preferred to layering because of phylloxera
P5: Oltre alla fillossera, perché si sceglie un portinnesto e che cosa sono incroci, ibridi e cloni? || En plus du phylloxéra, pourquoi choisit-on un porte-greffe et que sont croisements, hybrides et clones ? || Besides phylloxera, why choose a particular rootstock, and what are crossings, hybrids and clones?
K: rootstocks also protect against nematodes and give drought resistance, so used even where phylloxera is absent despite cost; crossing = new variety from two parents of the same species (technically all varieties; term reserved for bred varieties: Müller-Thurgau = Riesling × Madeleine Royale, Pinotage = Pinot Noir × Cinsault; Cabernet Sauvignon = Cabernet Franc × Sauvignon Blanc); hybrid = parents from two different species, usually an American parent; rarely used for wine (exception Vidal in Canada) but widely used as rootstocks; clone = vine or group with unique characteristics from mutation, selected and propagated (clonal selection) for quality or disease resistance; some mutations treated as new varieties (Pinot Blanc, Pinot Gris from Pinot Noir)

Q: Varietà, propagazione e anatomia della vite. || Cépages, multiplication et anatomie de la vigne. || Varieties, propagation and vine anatomy.
P5: Perché una varietà non si propaga da seme e come si propaga? Perché creare nuove varietà è lento e costoso? || Pourquoi un cépage ne se multiplie-t-il pas par semis et comment se multiplie-t-il ? Pourquoi créer de nouveaux cépages est-il long et coûteux ? || Why is a variety not propagated from seed, and how is it propagated? Why is breeding new varieties slow and costly?
K: a seed gives a new variety with different genetic material (even from parents of same variety); propagation by cuttings (section of shoot planted, used by nurseries) or layering (cane buried in vineyard, roots, then cut from parent) — new plant identical; variety = plants tracing back by cuttings/layering to a single plant; new varieties by cross-fertilisation (pollen to another flower, seed planted); unpredictable qualities, hundreds of seeds, many seedlings fail, 2–3 years before fruit, longer to prove value → few successful new varieties in last 150 years
P5: Descrivi le parti della vite (parti verdi, legno di un anno, legno permanente, radici) e le loro funzioni. || Décrivez les parties de la vigne (parties vertes, bois d'un an, bois permanent, racines) et leurs fonctions. || Describe the parts of the vine (green parts, one-year-old wood, permanent wood, roots) and their functions.
K: green parts grow each year: shoot with leaves (engine, photosynthesis: sunlight + water + CO2 → glucose + oxygen; glucose for growth and sweet grapes), buds (embryonic shoots in leaf/shoot join, contain next year's shoot, leaves, flowers, tendrils), tendrils (grip support), flowers in inflorescences (male and female parts) becoming berries/bunches; one-year-old wood: last year's shoots turned woody; fruit only on shoots from buds formed previous year; pruned to canes (8–20 buds) or spurs (2–3 buds); permanent wood: trunk and arms (cordons), limited by pruning; roots: absorb water and nutrients, anchor, store carbohydrates for winter; grafting: bench grafting (machine, nurseries, warm storage to fuse) and head grafting (change variety on established vine, crop next vintage, cheaper than replanting which needs 3 years)
