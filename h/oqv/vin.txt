Q: Vinificazione: principi di base. || Vinification : principes de base. || Winemaking: basic principles.
P6: Che cosa serve per la fermentazione alcolica e come incidono temperatura, lieviti e ossigeno? || Que faut-il pour la fermentation alcoolique et quel est l'effet de la température, des levures et de l'oxygène ? || What does alcoholic fermentation need, and what is the effect of temperature, yeast and oxygen?
K: yeast (mainly Saccharomyces cerevisiae, tolerates alcohol and SO2) converts sugar to alcohol and CO2, plus heat and flavour compounds; won't start below 5°C; may stop early if nutrients run out, temperature over 35°C, or very high sugar plus alcohol; winemaker can stop it by killing yeast (SO2, grape spirit) or chilling below 5°C and filtering; ambient yeasts: complexity but no control, batch variation (unsuitable for high volume); cultured yeasts: selected strains, consistent and attractive flavours, some say less complexity; lower temperatures keep volatile floral aromas and encourage fruity flavours in whites; higher temperatures extract colour and tannin from black skins; temperature control (jackets, coils, pumping over) huge contribution to quality and consistency; oxygen: protective/anaerobic winemaking (night picking, chilled grapes, SO2, equipment flushed with CO2 or nitrogen) keeps primary fruit; critics say bland — more oxygen builds complexity
P6: Spiega l'uso di SO2, l'aggiustamento di zucchero e acidità e il ruolo dei recipienti (acciaio, cemento, legno). || Expliquez l'usage du SO2, l'ajustement du sucre et de l'acidité et le rôle des contenants (inox, béton, bois). || Explain the use of SO2, sugar and acid adjustments and the role of vessels (stainless steel, concrete, oak).
K: SO2 antioxidant (becomes 'bound' so must be monitored and topped up) and antiseptic (toxic to spoilage yeasts and bacteria but S. cerevisiae tolerates it); legal limits (toxic, allergies); too much → harsh, lacking fruit; first dose on arrival at winery; enrichment with RCGM (cool climates, strictly controlled; abuse → hard, thin) or chaptalisation (non-grape sugar); removing water concentrates everything incl. faults and reduces volume; alcohol can be removed after fermentation; acidification with tartaric acid powder (EU warm regions only); deacidification with alkali in cool regions; inert vessels: stainless steel (easy clean, any shape, temperature control) and concrete (epoxy-lined, thick walls regulate temperature, harder to clean); oak: lets small amounts of oxygen in, softens tannins, tertiary development; adds tannin and flavour (toast, vanilla, smoke, cloves); hygiene challenge

Q: Maturazione, chiarifica e imbottigliamento. || Élevage, clarification et mise en bouteille. || Maturation, clarification and bottling.
P5: Confronta chiarifica (fining), filtrazione e stabilizzazione tartarica. || Comparez collage, filtration et stabilisation tartrique. || Compare fining, filtration and tartrate stabilisation.
K: clarification by sedimentation (gross lees settle in hours, fine lees slowly; racking; centrifuge expensive; for some fine wines the only clarification), fining (agent bonds with constituents forming clumps, then removed; prevents later hazes; some think it harms flavour/texture), filtration: depth filtration (thick layer, very cloudy wines, gross lees) and surface filtration (fine sieve, expensive, clogs, after depth; sterile filtration removes yeast and bacteria before bottling); some wines unfiltered; stability = changes slowly and predictably within its time frame; tartrates: tartaric acid less soluble in wine, harmless crystals (clear in whites, purple in reds) spoil appearance; long cool maturation deposits them, or chill below 0°C briefly and filter; microbiological stability (fortified safe; at-risk wines without MLF, low-medium alcohol, low acid, some sugar need SO2 and sterile filtration); oxygen stability (flush bottles with CO2/nitrogen, SO2)
P5: Che cosa cambia nel vino con l'affinamento in botte rispetto all'acciaio e quali chiusure esistono con i loro pro e contro? || Qu'apporte l'élevage en fût par rapport à l'inox et quelles fermetures existent avec leurs avantages et inconvénients ? || What does barrel maturation add compared with stainless steel, and which closures exist with their pros and cons?
K: inert airtight full vessels keep primary fruit (premium Australian Riesling like cheap White Zinfandel); oak watertight not airtight: small oxygen softens tannins, fruit fades, tertiary develops (leather, earth); reds paler, browner; whites deeper, hint of orange; oak effect depends on species/origin (European vs American), size (225 L barrique, 228 L pièce stronger than 2000 L+), toasting level (sweet spice, toast), age (new strongest, fourth use little flavour); rarely more than two years in barrique; part-filled vessels for deliberate oxidation (Oloroso, Tawny, Rutherglen Muscat); staves/chips cheaper oak flavour, micro-oxygenation; closures: cork (slow oxygen, balance primary/tertiary for ageing; TCA taint, variable oxygen ingress), synthetic (plastic, short-term, premium versions longer), screw cap (no taint, impermeable, preserves fruit longer, debate for long ageing, some allow oxygen; acceptance varies by market); closure is a winemaking choice; packaging: glass (inert, airtight, heavy), plastic bottles (air through, months), bag-in-box (about 18 months)

P5: Quali componenti dell'acino contano in vinificazione e perché? || Quels constituants du raisin comptent en vinification et pourquoi ? || Which parts of the grape matter in winemaking, and why?
K: bloom: waxy surface with yeasts that can ferment; skins and area beneath: flavour compounds (varietal character), tannins and colour (much more in black grapes); pulp: water (largest), sugar (second, used by yeast for alcohol), acids (tartaric most abundant, then malic); seeds: tannins and bitter oils; stems: tannins, only available if hand harvested; tannins very bitter/astringent at veraison, less as grapes ripen; oak also adds tannin
P5: Descrivi ricezione delle uve, diraspatura, pigiatura, pressatura e il motivo per separare le frazioni di pressa. || Décrivez réception, éraflage, foulage, pressurage et pourquoi séparer les fractions de presse. || Describe grape reception, destemming, crushing, pressing and why press fractions are separated.
K: SO2 on arrival; sorting tables for premium wines (rare in high volume); destemming optional (machine-harvested arrive without stems), usually done by machine that can then crush; crushing breaks skins, releases free run juice, must not crush seeds (bitter oils, tannin); pressing separates liquid from solids: whites before fermentation, reds usually after; gentle, seeds intact; vertical basket presses (still used, especially Champagne) vs pneumatic presses (inflatable bladder in horizontal cylinder, gentle controllable, some in closed tank to limit oxygen); fractions differ in flavour and texture, treated separately and blended for style; blending for balance (free run + press for tannin), consistency (barrels and vats differ), style (house style, MLF on part, different vessels)
