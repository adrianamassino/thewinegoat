How The Wine Goat Helps You Study for WSET

In short

The Wine Goat is an independent study app for the WSET wine and spirits qualifications. You choose your level, exam date and target grade; the app builds a daily plan of lessons, practice questions and flashcards, brings back your mistakes, lets you rehearse with mock exams in the real format, and keeps a diary of the wines and spirits you scan.

Updated 2026-11-18 · The Wine Goat

The Wine Goat turns a WSET syllabus into short daily sessions you can fit around work and life. Instead of deciding every evening what to study, you open the app and it tells you: today, this lesson, these questions, these cards. This page walks through how it works step by step, what each tool is for, who it suits and what it does not do.

What is The Wine Goat?

The Wine Goat is an exam-preparation and learning app for the WSET wine and spirits qualifications. It combines lessons, practice questions with explanations, flashcards, mock exams, games and a personal study plan in one place.

How does it work, step by step?

You set three things once (level, exam date, target grade), and the app turns them into a daily routine. Everything else follows from that plan.

1. Choose your level, exam date and goal

Pick the qualification you are preparing, enter the date of your exam and choose the result you are aiming for: a pass, or a merit or distinction where the level awards them. If you are unsure which level to choose, the guides to each level compare the format, content and study hours.

2. Get a daily plan

The app spreads the chapters across the weeks you have and sets a daily goal. The goal reflects the time left and the grade you are aiming for. Each day you see what to do next, so you never lose ten minutes deciding.

3. Learn with lessons, questions and flashcards

Each chapter starts with a short lesson. Then you practise with multiple-choice questions: every answer comes with a short explanation and a deeper section on why the other options are wrong, which is where most learning happens. Flashcards with spaced repetition fix the key facts: grapes, regions, label terms and numbers.

4. Your mistakes come back

Every question you get wrong goes into a review of your own mistakes, and the flashcards you miss return sooner than the ones you know. Instead of repeating what you already master, you spend time on the exact facts that keep tripping you up.

5. Rehearse with mock exams

When you have covered most of the syllabus, the plan adds mock exams that mirror the real format and timing. Afterwards, every wrong answer joins your mistakes review, so you can see where marks were lost and go back to those lessons.

6. Keep a diary of what you taste

Point your phone at a label or a glass and the scanner adds the wine or spirit to your tasting diary, in 'My wines' or 'My spirits'. Linking what you study to what you actually drink is one of the most effective memory aids there is: the Rioja you had at dinner becomes a real example of Tempranillo, oak and ageing terms.

Who is The Wine Goat for?

It is for anyone preparing a WSET wine or spirits exam who wants a clear daily routine and plenty of practice, whether you work in the trade or study for pleasure.

  • Beginners taking Level 1 or Level 2 who want to arrive at the exam calm and well practised.
  • Restaurant, bar, shop and wholesale staff fitting study around shifts, in ten- or twenty-minute sessions.
  • Level 3 candidates who need structure for a large syllabus, written-answer practice and tasting discipline.
  • Enthusiasts who simply want to learn wine or spirits properly, with or without an exam date.
  • Learners who prefer to study in Italian, French, English or Spanish (Spain and Argentina).

It is not a replacement for your course. Classes give you a tutor, guided tastings and the exam itself; the app gives you the practice and repetition between those sessions.

A typical day with the app

On a normal day, a Level 2 learner might spend about half an hour in short blocks rather than one long sitting.

  • Morning, 8 minutes: review the flashcards due today.
  • Lunch break, 10 minutes: read the next lesson in the plan, for example the Loire Valley.
  • Evening, 12 minutes: 20 practice questions on that chapter, reading the explanation for each mistake.
  • Weekend: one timed mock exam, then the mistakes review on the weakest chapters.

The daily goal is met, the streak continues, and the chapter progress bar moves. Over six weeks, that adds up to the guideline study time without a single marathon evening.

Can you study in your own language?

Yes. The app is available in Italian, French, English and Spanish, with separate versions for Spain and Argentina, so you can learn in the language you think in.

Many candidates study in one language and sit the exam in another, or take a course abroad. Being able to switch lets you understand a difficult concept, such as malolactic conversion or the solera system, in your own language first, then review the same chapter in the language of your exam so the key terms are familiar on the day. Reviewing in the exam language matters because the exam rewards precise terms rather than synonyms.

If your exam is in English but you study more easily in another language, learn each chapter in your language and do the practice questions and mock exams in English.

Common mistakes when studying with an app

An app makes practice easy, but it can also make it too comfortable. These habits keep it effective.

  • Rushing through questions without reading the explanations: the 'why the other options are wrong' section is where you learn the traps.
  • Repeating only favourite chapters because scores feel good there.
  • Skipping the mistakes review, so the same errors return on exam day.
  • Avoiding mock exams until the final week.
  • For Level 3, practising only multiple choice and neglecting written answers and tasting.

How the mistakes review and spaced repetition work together

The app keeps two memories of your weak points: the questions you got wrong and the flashcards you missed. Both are brought back on a schedule, so your time is spent on what you have not yet mastered rather than on what you already know.

When you miss a question, it is saved to your personal review with its explanation and the reasons why the other options are wrong. When you return to it a few days later and answer correctly, it moves further away; if you miss it again, it comes back sooner. Flashcards follow the same logic with their own intervals. Over a few weeks, the review becomes a precise map of your remaining gaps, and that map is exactly what you need in the final phase before the exam.

Using the Wine Level 3 tools

Wine Level 3 adds three kinds of support for the parts of the exam that multiple-choice questions cannot reach: written answers, tasting and the geography and techniques behind each region.

Open questions with model answers

Each of the 101 open questions asks you to explain, describe or compare, as a short written answer does. Write your answer first, then compare it with the model answer and the marking points. Count the ideas you stated clearly with their reasons. The gap between your answer and the marking points shows which explanations you still need to learn.

Blind-tasting trainer

The trainer guides you through the systematic approach for each wine you taste: appearance, nose, palate and conclusions, using the exam's vocabulary and scales. Use it with any wine you open, ideally poured by someone else so you do not see the label, and you build the habit of complete, consistent notes.

Maps, films and podcasts

Maps make regions easier to place and connect with their climate: a river, a coast or a mountain range often explains the style of the wine. Films and podcasts help you picture vineyard and cellar techniques and are useful when you cannot sit down with a lesson, for example while travelling.

Getting the most from the scanner and tasting diary

Use the diary as revision, not just as a record: every saved bottle is a chance to connect a label with what you studied. A few seconds of reflection per entry turns everyday drinking into practice.

  • Before tasting, read the label and predict the style: grape, climate, likely acidity and body.
  • Taste, then compare the wine with your prediction and note what surprised you.
  • Link the entry to its chapter, for example a Chianti to Italy or a Bourbon to American whiskeys.
  • Before the exam, scroll through "My wines" or "My spirits" and recall each region and category: a personal set of revision prompts.

Three learners, three ways of using the app

  • A sommelier assistant preparing Wine Level 3 uses the open questions every other day, tastes with the trainer during staff tastings and keeps maps open while studying regions.
  • A shop employee preparing Spirits Level 2 reviews flashcards between customers, scans each new bottle on the shelf into "My spirits" and sits mock exams on days off.
  • A wine lover with no exam date uses Wine Level 1 for free, keeps a streak with games and lessons, and switches to Level 2 when ready to book an exam.

Whatever your profile, open the app once a day, even for five minutes. The plan, the streak and the spaced-repetition schedule all work best with daily contact.

Frequently asked questions

Is The Wine Goat an official WSET app?

No. The Wine Goat is an independent exam-preparation app. It is not run by, affiliated with or endorsed by the Wine & Spirit Education Trust, and it is not an Approved Programme Provider. You enrol on your course and sit your exam with an approved provider, and use the app to study and practise alongside it.

Which WSET levels does The Wine Goat cover?

Wine Levels 1, 2 and 3 and Spirits Levels 1 and 2 are available today. Wine Level 1 and Spirits Level 1 are free. Spirits Level 3 and the Diploma are in preparation. Each available level includes lessons, practice questions with explanations, flashcards, mock exams, games and a personal study plan.

How does the study plan work?

You choose your level, enter your exam date and pick a target grade. The app spreads the chapters across the time you have and sets a daily goal. Each day it shows what to do next: a lesson, practice questions, flashcards, a game or a mock exam. Progress per chapter and a streak show how you are doing.

What happens to the questions I get wrong?

They go into a review of your own mistakes, so you can work on them specifically, and the flashcards you miss come back sooner thanks to spaced repetition. Each question also explains why the right answer is right and why the other options are wrong, so you understand the trap rather than just memorising a letter.

Are the mock exams like the real exam?

They mirror the real format and timing: 30 questions in 45 minutes for Level 1, 50 questions in 60 minutes for Level 2, and 50 multiple-choice questions plus 4 short written answers for the Wine Level 3 theory paper. The questions are written by The Wine Goat and are not official exam questions.

What is the label and glass scanner for?

It lets you add the wines and spirits you taste to a personal diary, 'My wines' or 'My spirits', by photographing the label or the glass. Connecting real bottles to what you study, such as grape, region, label terms and style, makes the theory far easier to remember on exam day.

Can I use The Wine Goat without an exam date?

Yes. You can study the lessons, questions, flashcards and games for any available level at your own pace, which suits enthusiasts who want to learn wine or spirits properly without sitting an exam yet. The personal study plan works best with a date, though, because it spreads the chapters across the weeks and sets the daily goal. A provisional date is a good compromise.

How much time do I need to spend in the app each day?

It depends on your level and target grade. As a guide, about 15–20 minutes for Level 1, around 30 minutes for Level 2 and 45–60 minutes for Level 3, split into short sessions if that suits you. The app sets a daily goal from your exam date and target grade; meeting it consistently matters more than occasional long sessions.

Does The Wine Goat replace my WSET course?

No. You still enrol with a WSET course provider, who teaches the course, runs guided tastings and organises the exam. The Wine Goat is an independent study app that helps you remember and practise what the course teaches, with lessons, practice questions, flashcards, mock exams and a study plan between and after your classes.

What are the games in the app for?

The games, survival, match-it and lightning quiz, train fast recall in a lighter format. They are ideal for short breaks or tired evenings when a full lesson feels like too much, and they keep your streak going. They complement the core work of lessons, questions, flashcards and mock exams rather than replacing it.

Can I practise the Level 3 tasting in the app without opening wine?

Partly. The blind-tasting trainer teaches the structure of the systematic approach, the scales and the vocabulary, and you can learn those away from the glass with flashcards and lessons. The tasting unit, though, tests what you perceive in real wines, so the trainer is most useful when you have a glass in front of you. Aim to combine both: vocabulary on the go, full notes whenever you taste.

Does The Wine Goat work for spirits as well as wine?

Yes. Spirits Level 1 is free with about 210 questions, and Spirits Level 2 has about 1,000 questions, each with lessons by chapter, flashcards, mock exams in the real format, games and a study plan. The scanner works for spirits too, saving bottles to "My spirits". Spirits Level 3 is in preparation.
