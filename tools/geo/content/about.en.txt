## What is The Wine Goat?
The Wine Goat is an exam-preparation app for the WSET wine and spirits qualifications, with a public website of free guides. The app works in a web browser on phone, tablet or computer, and the website explains the exams, the study method and the features of the app in several languages.
The name comes from the brand's mascots: a family of illustrated goats that appear throughout the site and the app, each in a different wine situation, from tasting a glass to studying a map. They are there to make long study sessions a little lighter; the content behind them is serious exam preparation.
The project grew from a simple observation. Most candidates for a WSET exam attend a course, read the course book and then face the same problem: how to turn hundreds of pages of grapes, regions, laws, winemaking and tasting into knowledge they can recall under time pressure. The Wine Goat exists to fill that gap between reading and passing, with active recall, spaced repetition, realistic practice and a plan that fits into a working week.

## Who is The Wine Goat for?
The app is designed for anyone preparing a WSET wine or spirits exam who wants to practise actively instead of only re-reading notes. In practice, that covers a wide range of learners with different starting points.
- Wine lovers taking their first qualification, usually Level 1 or Level 2, who want to understand the basics of grapes, styles, labels and food pairing without feeling lost.
- Hospitality professionals (sommeliers, waiting staff, bartenders, retail and wine shop staff) who need a qualification for work and have little free time between shifts.
- Level 3 candidates facing a much larger syllabus, short written answers and a blind tasting, who need structure, repetition and realistic exam practice.
- Spirits learners preparing Spirits Level 1 or Level 2, from production methods to the main categories and their labelling.
- Candidates who study in one language and sit the exam in another, or who want to learn in the language they think in.
- Teachers and wine schools that want to give students an extra practice tool between classes (there is a dedicated page for schools).
It is also useful after the exam. Many people keep using the maps, the flashcards and the tasting diary simply to keep what they learned fresh.
The Wine Goat is less useful if you are looking for a recognised certificate on its own: the app helps you prepare, but certificates are issued only by the exam body after an exam taken through an approved course provider.

## Our educational mission
The mission of The Wine Goat is to make serious wine and spirits study accessible, efficient and enjoyable, in the learner's own language, so that more people walk into their exam prepared.
That mission translates into a few practical principles that shape every feature.
- Active recall over passive reading. Answering a question forces your memory to work; re-reading a page mostly creates a feeling of familiarity. Every topic in the app ends in practice.
- Explanations, not just answers. A score tells you where you are; an explanation tells you why. Every multiple-choice question explains the correct answer and why the other options are wrong.
- Little and often. Short daily sessions, spread over weeks, beat long evenings before the exam. The study planner is built around minutes per day.
- Your mistakes are your syllabus. Questions and flashcards you miss come back sooner, and a dedicated review collects your errors.
- Realistic rehearsal. Mock exams reproduce the number of questions, the timing and the pass marks of the real papers, so the format is familiar on the day.
- No dead ends for beginners. Wine Level 1 is free in full, and every level has a free starting point, so anyone can try the method before committing time to it.
- Respect for learners' languages. Wine vocabulary is international, but understanding is not: explanations work best in the language you think in.

## Which qualifications does The Wine Goat cover?
The app covers five WSET qualifications today: Wine Levels 1, 2 and 3 and Spirits Levels 1 and 2. Spirits Level 3 and the Diploma in Wines are in preparation and are not yet available. The table below shows what exists now; it is updated when a level is added.
| Qualification | Status in the app | What you find |
| Wine Level 1 | Available, completely free | Lessons, practice questions with explanations, flashcards, mock exam in the real format, study plan |
| Wine Level 2 | Available | About 1,070 practice questions, lessons by chapter, flashcards, mock exams, grape and region tools |
| Wine Level 3 | Available | 2,000+ multiple-choice questions, 101 short written questions with model answers and marking points, blind-tasting trainer, interactive maps, films, podcasts |
| Spirits Level 1 | Available, free | About 210 practice questions, lessons, flashcards, mock exam |
| Spirits Level 2 | Available | About 1,000 practice questions, lessons, flashcards, mock exams |
| Spirits Level 3 | In preparation | Not yet available |
| Diploma (Level 4) | In preparation | Not yet available; the site has a study-tips guide |
The exam formats the app reproduces are the published ones: at Level 1 (wine or spirits) 30 multiple-choice questions in 45 minutes with a 70% pass mark; at Level 2, 50 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes with 55% to pass, 70% for Merit and 85% for Distinction; at Wine Level 3, a theory paper of 50 multiple-choice questions plus 4 short written-answer questions, and a blind tasting of two wines described with the systematic approach to tasting.
! The Wine Goat never promises a pass and never calls its questions "official". They are written to match the style and the level of difficulty of the real exam, using the published specification of each qualification as the frame.

## What can you do in the app?
The app brings together the tools a candidate normally collects from different places: notes, question banks, flashcards, maps, a timer for mock exams and a calendar. Everything is linked to the same progress, so what you get wrong in one tool comes back in the others.
### Lessons
Each level is divided into chapters that follow the syllabus. Lessons are short and written to be read on a phone: one idea at a time, with the key facts highlighted and a set of questions at the end of each chapter.
### Practice questions with explanations
Multiple-choice questions in the style of the exam, organised by topic. After each answer you see a short explanation and a deeper "why the other options are wrong" note, which is often where the real learning happens.
### Flashcards with spaced repetition
Cards for grapes, regions, label terms, numbers and production steps. Cards you know are spaced further apart; cards you miss come back sooner, so your time goes where it is needed.
### Mock exams
Timed papers that mirror the real format, number of questions and pass marks of each level, with a result by topic at the end, so you know what to revise next.
### Short-answer marking
At Wine Level 3, 101 short written questions with model answers. You write your answer and it is marked against the key points of the model answer, showing which points you made and which you missed.
### Tasting trainer
A trainer for the systematic approach to tasting used in the Level 3 blind tasting: you practise describing appearance, nose and palate and reaching conclusions on quality, in the structure the examiners expect.
### Interactive maps
More than 40 wine maps, from France, Italy and Spain to Chile, New Zealand and South Africa, with an atlas to explore and a map game with three difficulty levels. A dedicated page explains how the wine maps work.
### Animated films and podcasts
Narrated animated films on processes that are easier to see than to read (in the vineyard, in the winery, sparkling wines, Sherry, Port, service) and podcast lessons to listen to, topic by topic, with a written transcript.
### Study planner
You enter your level, exam date, target grade (Pass, Merit or Distinction) and how many minutes a day you can study; the app builds a day-by-day plan and adapts it as you progress.
### Label scanner and tasting diary
Point your phone at a label or a glass and the scanner adds the wine or spirit to your diary, "My wines" or "My spirits", so what you drink becomes examples for what you study.
### Games and level test
Short games (survival, match-it, lightning quiz) for days with little energy, and a test of your level that tells you where you stand, what to strengthen and how many days of study you need. See the page on the wine knowledge test for details.

## How does the app work, day to day?
You set three things once (level, exam date and target grade) and the app turns them into a routine of short sessions. A typical day takes between 15 and 30 minutes.
1. Open the app and look at today's plan: a lesson or two, a set of practice questions and a few flashcards.
2. Study the lesson, then answer the questions on that chapter. Read every explanation, including those for answers you got right.
3. Review your mistakes. Questions you missed are collected in one place and come back over the following days.
4. Do your flashcards. The spaced-repetition queue decides which cards you see.
5. Once a week, or more often near the exam, sit a mock exam in exam conditions and read the result by topic.
6. When you taste a wine or a spirit, scan it and add a short note to your diary.
Progress is shown per chapter, with streaks to keep the habit going. If you create a free account, your progress is saved to your profile and synced, so you can switch between phone and computer. The detailed step-by-step walk-through is on the "How it works" page.

## Multilingual by design
The Wine Goat was built in several languages from the start, because many candidates study in a language that is not English and because explanations are understood best in your own language.
- The app interface is available in 14 languages.
- The full study content (lessons, questions, explanations, flashcards) is available in Italian, French, English and Spanish, with a separate Argentine Spanish version.
- The website's guides are published in many more languages, so learners can read about the exams and the study method in their own language before opening the app.
- Each level shows a notice when the exam of that level is not offered in the language you have chosen, and suggests a language in which it is, so you never prepare in a language you cannot sit the exam in.
Wine terms that appear on labels (appellations, quality categories, grape names) are kept in their original form, because that is how they appear in the exam and on the bottle, and they are explained in the language of the app.

## How is the content written and checked?
Every lesson and question starts from the published specification of the qualification it prepares for, and every fact is checked against reference sources before it goes into the app. The aim is accuracy first, then clarity.
The editorial approach follows a few rules that are applied to every level and every language.
- Syllabus first. Content is organised by the chapters and learning outcomes of each qualification, so you study what the exam can ask and not much more.
- Own wording. Lessons and questions are written in The Wine Goat's own words. The course book of the exam body is not copied; it remains the reference text you receive with your course.
- One fact, one source of truth. Numbers that recur (pass marks, exam timings, ageing rules, minimum alcohol levels) are kept consistent between lessons, questions, flashcards and website guides.
- Explanations for every answer. Each question explains why the right answer is right and why the other options are wrong, which also helps catch ambiguous questions.
- Corrections everywhere. When an error is found, by a learner or during a review, it is corrected in the master text and in every language version, on the app and on the website.
- Translations aligned to a master. Language versions are prepared from a master text (with the help of translation tools, including AI) and then aligned on terminology, so that a term means the same thing in every language.
- No invented claims. The site does not publish pass rates, testimonials, ratings or statistics about users, and it never presents its questions as official exam questions.
If you spot something that looks wrong, write to hello@thewinegoat.com with the page or the question: reports are read and fixed.

## Independence from WSET
The Wine Goat is independent. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by the Wine & Spirit Education Trust, and it is not an Approved Programme Provider.
WSET® is a registered trademark of the Wine & Spirit Education Trust. It is mentioned on this site only to identify the exams that The Wine Goat helps you prepare for. To sit an exam you need to book a course and the exam with an approved course provider; the certificate is issued by the exam body, not by this app.
Being independent has two practical consequences. First, the questions are original practice questions, not past papers. Second, the study method is The Wine Goat's own, and you should always follow your course provider's instructions about the exam itself (dates, rules, materials allowed).

## What The Wine Goat does not do
Clarity about limits is part of being useful. The Wine Goat:
- does not issue certificates or qualifications;
- does not book exams or courses;
- does not replace your course or the course book;
- does not publish real exam papers or claim to know what will be in your exam;
- does not guarantee a result: practice improves your chances, it cannot make them certain.
