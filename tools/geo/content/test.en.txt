## What is the wine knowledge test?
It is a level check made of practice questions in the style of the WSET wine exams, chosen to cover every topic of the syllabus rather than one chapter. It is not a mock exam: its job is to measure where you are, not to rehearse the timing of the day.
There are three ways to take it, from the home page of the site or from inside the app.
| Format | Questions | Time | What it measures |
| Quick challenge | 20 multiple-choice questions across the whole syllabus | About 10 minutes | A fast first picture of your level |
| Level session | 50 mixed multiple-choice questions, like the theory paper | About 30 minutes | A reliable score and a breakdown by topic |
| Written level session | 3 short written-answer questions, marked | About 15–20 minutes | How well you turn knowledge into marks in written answers |
The questions come from the bank of the level you are studying in the app and are spread across its chapters, so a test is never just Bordeaux or just winemaking.

## How does the test work, step by step?
You answer the questions, create a free account if you do not have one, and the result is shown and saved in your profile. The whole process is designed to take less time than a coffee break for the quick version.
1. Start the test: "Take the challenge" on the site opens the 20-question quick challenge; the level session and the written session are offered next to it.
2. Answer each question without notes, the way you would in the exam, and read the explanations to learn from each answer.
3. At the end, the app tells you that your result is ready. To see it, create a free account (or sign in). The result stays saved in your profile, so you can compare later tests with it.
4. Read the result: your score, your level, your topics from weakest to strongest and your forecast.
5. Choose what to do next: build a day-by-day plan from the result, start a new session, or go straight to the weakest topic.
! For an honest result, take the test without notes and without looking anything up. A guessed answer that happens to be right hides a gap you will meet again in the exam.

## What does the result show?
The result is more than a score. It turns your answers into a level, a list of priorities and a realistic estimate of the study time you need.
### Your score and your level
You see the percentage of correct answers (or of marks, for the written session) and a level based on it.
| Score | Level shown in the app | What it means for your plan |
| Below 40% | Beginner level | Start from the basics: the plan follows the syllabus chapter by chapter, with plenty of practice |
| 40% to 69% | Intermediate level | Good foundations: the plan focuses on the topics you got wrong |
| 70% and above | Advanced level | Well on your way: more mock exams and written questions, with targeted revision |
### Your topics, weakest first
Each topic of the syllabus covered by your answers gets its own bar with a percentage. They are sorted from weakest to strongest, so the first line of the list is the first thing to study.
### Your forecast
After the quick challenge, the forecast estimates the score you would get if you sat the exam today, reminds you of the pass mark, and shows how many days of study you need to reach a Pass, a Merit and a Distinction at your daily pace. You can change the pace (minutes per day) and the estimate updates. If you have set an exam date, the app tells you whether you will make it comfortably or whether it is tight, and how many minutes a day would get you there.
### What to do, in this order
The forecast ends with a short to-do list built from your answers. For example: strengthen your weakest topic with 10 questions a day until you pass 70% on it; practise one written question every two days, because in the exam written answers count as much as multiple choice; from a given week, sit one full mock exam a week; and every day, do 10 questions, your mistake review and a quick game.

## How is the study-time estimate calculated?
The estimate combines your current score with the distance to each grade and the number of minutes a day you can study. It is an estimate, not a promise, and the app says so: it is based on the number of questions you have answered, and it becomes more precise the more you answer.
In practice, three things move it.
- Your score. The closer you already are to the grade you want, the fewer days you need.
- Your pace. Fifteen minutes a day and forty-five minutes a day lead to very different dates; you can try both.
- Your target. A Distinction needs a much higher score than a Pass, so it needs more days.
Treat the number of days as a planning tool. If the estimate lands after your exam date, you know now, not the week before, that you need more minutes a day, a later exam date or a sharper focus on your weakest topics.

## What does a written question look like?
The written level session asks three short-answer questions and marks them against the key points of a model answer. Here is an example from the app, worth 5 marks.
Question: Wine B has a natural cork closure. What storage conditions would you recommend for keeping this wine long term?
The marking points include: storing the bottle lying on its side so the cork stays moist and does not dry out and let in oxygen; a cool, constant temperature (about 10–15 °C) without fluctuations that expand the wine and push the cork; darkness, because light and especially UV damage wine; moderate humidity; and a place away from vibration and strong smells.
Each point you make earns a mark. The marking shows which points you made and which you missed, which is exactly what you need to learn how examiners read an answer: precise, separate points beat a long, vague paragraph.

## Who should take the wine knowledge test?
Anyone preparing a WSET wine exam can use it, at several moments of their preparation.
- Before choosing a level. If you are unsure between Level 2 and Level 3, a test on the Level 2 bank shows whether you already have the foundations.
- At the start of a course. A first result gives you a baseline and a list of topics to watch while the course goes on.
- Halfway through your preparation. A second test shows what has improved and whether your plan still fits your exam date.
- Three to four weeks before the exam. At this point, the level session and the written session tell you whether to spend your remaining time on mock exams or on specific topics.
- After a break. If you stopped studying for a few weeks, a quick challenge shows what has faded.
It is also a good way to try the app itself: in ten minutes you see the style of the questions, the explanations and the way the app turns answers into a plan.

## Why the questions are spread across the whole syllabus
A level test is only useful if it samples every part of what the exam can ask. That is why the app does not pick questions at random from the whole bank, where one large chapter could fill half the test: it spreads them across the chapters of the syllabus, so viticulture, winemaking, the main regions, fortified and sparkling wines and service all appear.
This has two consequences for how you read the result. First, a weak bar on one topic usually rests on only a few questions, so treat it as a signal to check, not a verdict; a second test or a short practice set on that topic confirms it. Second, a strong overall score with one very weak topic is still a risk, because the real exam also samples every chapter. The aim before the exam is not a high average but no topic left behind.

## Challenge a friend
After any practice session, you can send the same set of questions to a friend. They receive a challenge with your score, answer the same questions and see who did better. Studying with someone from your course, even at a distance, is one of the simplest ways to keep going when motivation drops.

## Reading a result: three examples
The same score can call for very different plans, depending on how it is spread across topics and how much time is left. These three examples show how to read a result.
### 62% overall, ten weeks to the exam
An intermediate result with plenty of time. The topic list shows strong winemaking and Bordeaux, but weak Germany and fortified wines. The right move is not more of everything: it is ten questions a day on the two weak topics until each passes 70%, flashcards for the label terms of Germany, and a first mock exam in about four weeks.
### 48% overall, four weeks to the exam
Close to the boundary and short on time. The forecast will usually say it is tight and suggest more minutes a day. Here the priority is breadth: one lesson and one set of questions per chapter, in syllabus order, with the mistakes review every day. Written questions start in week two, one every two days.
### 81% overall, two weeks to the exam
An advanced result. Multiple choice is no longer the main risk; written answers and timing are. The plan shifts to full mock exams, written questions with marking points, and short sessions on the two or three topics that still sit below 70%.

## Turning your result into a plan
The app builds the plan for you, but it helps to know the logic behind it, so you can adjust it to your week.
| Weeks to the exam | Main focus after the test | Mock exams |
| More than 8 | Work through the syllabus chapter by chapter, weakest topics first, with daily questions and flashcards | One every few weeks to measure progress |
| 4 to 8 | Fix the weakest topics until each is above 70%; start written questions | One every two weeks, then weekly |
| 2 to 4 | Mixed practice across every topic; written questions every two days | Weekly, in exam conditions |
| Less than 2 | Mistakes review, short targeted sessions, rest before the day | One or two, then stop and consolidate |
Repeat the level session at each change of phase. If your weakest topic has moved, your plan should move with it.

## Common mistakes when testing your level
- Taking the test once and never again. The value is in the comparison: test at the start, halfway and near the exam.
- Reading only the score. The topic list is the useful part; the first line tells you what to study tomorrow.
- Testing with notes open. It feels better and tells you nothing.
- Ignoring written answers. Multiple-choice knowledge does not automatically turn into written marks; the written session shows the gap.
- Treating the estimate as a promise. It is a planning tool that becomes more precise as you answer more questions.
