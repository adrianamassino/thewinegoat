WSET Level 3 Red and rosé wines: practice questions with answers

These are real questions from The Wine Goat bank on Red and rosé wines, written to the WSET Level 3 Award in Wines syllabus. Answer them, read the explanation, then practise the full set of 102 questions in the free app, where your mistakes come back until you know them.

Sample questions with answers

1 In red winemaking, when does pressing normally happen?

Show the answer

Answer: After fermentation

Why: Skins stay in the vat to extract colour and tannin. In reds MLF is standard practice.

2 What is the purpose of cold maceration before fermentation?

Show the answer

Answer: To extract colour and aroma compounds from the skins

Why: Tannins are more soluble in alcohol, so little is extracted before fermentation.

3 What is the usual fermentation temperature range for reds?

Show the answer

Answer: 20-32 °C

Why: Warmer than whites to aid extraction; above 35 °C yeast may die. Cooling near the end reduces tannin extraction.

4 What is the cap?

Show the answer

Answer: The mass of skins and pulp that rises to the top during fermentation

Why: A floating cap gives little extraction, hence cap management techniques.

5 What is the risk of over-vigorous punching down late in fermentation?

Show the answer

Answer: Too much tannin: a bitter, astringent wine

Why: Tannins extract more easily late on; punching down is very effective for colour and tannin.

6 In red winemaking, malolactic fermentation is:

Show the answer

Answer: Standard practice (virtually all reds undergo it)

Why: Reds: MLF standard; whites: a stylistic choice.
