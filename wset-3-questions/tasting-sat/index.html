WSET Level 3 Tasting and the SAT: practice questions with answers

These are real questions from The Wine Goat bank on Tasting and the SAT, written to the WSET Level 3 Award in Wines syllabus. Answer them, read the explanation, then practise the full set of 146 questions in the free app, where your mistakes come back until you know them.

Sample questions with answers

1 What two fundamental skills is the SAT designed to develop?

Show the answer

Answer: Describing a wine accurately and drawing reasoned conclusions

Why: The SAT is a teaching and assessment tool: it describes (appearance, nose, palate) and then concludes (quality and readiness).

2 What are the two headings in the 'Conclusions' section of the SAT?

Show the answer

Answer: Quality level and readiness for drinking/ageing potential

Why: In the Conclusions you must use the listed terms: quality level and readiness for drinking.

3 Where is the Wine-Lexicon found?

Show the answer

Answer: On the reverse of the laminated SAT card

Why: The Lexicon lists aroma and flavour descriptors to help describe a wine; it is on the back of the SAT card.

4 A wine sits between ruby and garnet. How should you describe it in the exam?

Show the answer

Answer: Choose just one term, the closer one

Why: On hyphenated lines you pick one term only; a range such as 'ruby to garnet' scores no mark.

5 In the SAT 'Conclusions' section, may you use your own words?

Show the answer

Answer: No: only the terms listed in the SAT

Why: The Conclusions are the evaluative part of the SAT, with two headings: quality level and readiness for drinking/potential for ageing. You are restricted to the specific listed terms here (e.g. 'good', 'can drink now, but has potential for ageing'). Freedom to use other words applies only to the 'e.g.' lines (aroma and flavour characteristics).

6 At Level 3, how have many three-point scales been refined?

Show the answer

Answer: By splitting 'medium' into medium(-), medium and medium(+)

Why: Only the 'medium' band is split, because most wines fall there: it is not five equal parts.
