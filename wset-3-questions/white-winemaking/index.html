WSET Level 3 White wines: practice questions with answers

These are real questions from The Wine Goat bank on White wines, written to the WSET Level 3 Award in Wines syllabus. Answer them, read the explanation, then practise the full set of 104 questions in the free app, where your mistakes come back until you know them.

Sample questions with answers

1 Why does white juice usually spend little time on the skins?

Show the answer

Answer: To reduce the risk of oxidation

Why: Whole-bunch pressing limits contact further, gently, potentially giving purer, more delicate wines.

2 Why is short skin contact sometimes used for aromatic varieties?

Show the answer

Answer: To increase flavour intensity and texture

Why: It usually lasts a few hours, cool enough to prevent fermentation.

3 What is the optimum fermentation temperature for whites?

Show the answer

Answer: 12-22 °C

Why: Too cold gives pear drop and loses varietal fruit; warmer gives complex non-fruit aromas but loses fruit.

4 What aroma forms if a white is fermented too cold?

Show the answer

Answer: Pear drop (estery)

Why: Too cold a fermentation can also fail to capture varietal fruit.

5 At what temperature do barrel fermentations usually run?

Show the answer

Answer: At the higher end of the 12–22 °C range

Why: Barrels are hard to cool but, small and in cool cellars, dissipate heat well.

6 What is the main role of blending in aromatic, primary-fruit whites?

Show the answer

Answer: Ensuring consistency rather than adding complexity

Why: Many whites rely on pure primary fruit, so blending focuses on consistency. For non-aromatic varieties like Chardonnay the reverse may apply: batches given different lees, MLF and oak treatment are blended for a more complex style.
