WSET Level 2 grapes and regions: the cheat sheet

In short

WSET Level 2 focuses on about 15 principal grapes and the regions where they make their best-known wines. For each grape, learn its key regions, its typical style and how a cool or warm climate changes it.

Updated 2026-11-15 · The Wine Goat

Most WSET Level 2 questions link a grape to a region and a style. Learn this table well and you answer a large part of the exam.

Cool vs warm climate

Cool climates give higher acidity, lighter body and green or citrus fruit; warm climates give riper, riper-tasting fruit, more body and more alcohol. Many questions ask exactly this contrast for Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Syrah and Cabernet Sauvignon.

Regional grapes often asked

Albariño (Rías Baixas), Grüner Veltliner (Austria), Furmint (Tokaj), Torrontés (Argentina), Pinotage (South Africa), Carmenère (Chile), Zinfandel (California), Garganega (Soave), Montepulciano (Abruzzo).

How to memorise them

  • Group grapes by colour and by climate preference.
  • Practise in both directions: grape → region and region → grape.
  • Ten minutes of mixed questions every day beats one long session.

Frequently asked questions

How many grapes do you need to know for WSET Level 2?

The course focuses on a group of principal international grapes plus a number of regionally important ones; the table above covers the main ones.

What is the best way to learn WSET Level 2 regions?

Use a map and practise grape ↔ region questions daily. The Wine Goat has interactive maps and flashcards for this.

Is there tasting in the WSET Level 2 exam?

No: the Level 2 exam is multiple choice only, though tasting in class helps you remember the styles.
