The WSET Level 3 exam, explained part by part

The WSET Level 3 Award in Wines is assessed in two units: a theory paper and a blind tasting. You need at least 55% in each unit to pass, and a strong result in one cannot make up for a weak result in the other.

Knowing exactly what each part asks for is the fastest way to stop losing marks. Here is how the exam is built and how to train for each section.

Unit 1 – Theory: 50 multiple-choice questions

The multiple-choice section covers the whole syllabus: the vineyard, winemaking, the regions of the world, sparkling and fortified wines, storage, service and food pairing. Each question has one correct answer among four options, and many options are deliberately plausible.

  • Expect questions on causes and effects (why a climate gives a style), not only on names.
  • Wrong options often mix a real grape with the wrong region, or a real technique with the wrong effect.
  • Practise in timed sets: speed comes from recognising patterns, not from reading faster.

Unit 1 – Theory: 4 short written answers

The written paper is where most marks are won or lost. Each question is split into parts, and every mark rewards one correct, explained idea. Lists of keywords without the “why” score poorly.

  • Use one idea per sentence and always link cause → effect → style.
  • Look at the marks per part: 6 marks usually means 6 separate points.
  • Use precise exam vocabulary (e.g. “diurnal range”, “malolactic conversion”, “lees contact”).

Unit 2 – Tasting: two wines, blind

You taste two wines and describe them with the Systematic Approach to Tasting: appearance, nose, palate and conclusions on quality and readiness for drinking. Marks come from accurate, consistent descriptions; you are not asked to name the wine.

  • Write a full note for every structural element: sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol, body, flavour intensity, finish.
  • Justify quality with balance, length, intensity and complexity.
  • Train with the official terms only – synonyms do not score.

Results and grades

Each unit is marked separately. With 55% or more in both units you pass; higher overall results give a Merit or a Distinction. Results usually arrive a few weeks after the exam.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the WSET Level 3 exam?

The theory paper (multiple choice plus short written answers) and the tasting are taken on the same exam day; the theory paper lasts about two hours and the tasting is short. Your school confirms the exact timetable.

What is the pass mark for WSET Level 3?

You need at least 55% in the theory unit and at least 55% in the tasting unit. The two units are marked separately.

Can I retake only one unit?

Yes: if you pass one unit and fail the other, you normally retake only the unit you failed. Check the details with your course provider.
