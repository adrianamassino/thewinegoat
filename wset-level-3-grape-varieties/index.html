The grape varieties to know for WSET Level 3

At Level 3 knowing the name of a grape is not enough: you have to explain why it tastes the way it does in a given place. Each variety reacts to climate, soil and winemaking choices, and the exam rewards that reasoning.

Start from the international varieties below, then add the local grapes of each region as you study it.

How to study grapes the Level 3 way

  • Learn each grape as a profile: colour, acidity, tannin, typical aromas, how it responds to oak.
  • Link it to climate: the same grape in a cool and a warm region gives two different wines – know both.
  • Add the winemaking: e.g. Chardonnay with or without malolactic conversion, lees and new oak.
  • Test yourself with mixed questions: grape → region, region → grape, style → grape.

Local grapes not to forget

Examiners love regional grapes: Albariño (Rías Baixas), Verdejo (Rueda), Assyrtiko (Santorini), Grüner Veltliner (Austria), Furmint (Tokaj), Torrontés (Salta), Pinotage (South Africa), Carmenère (Chile), Zinfandel (California), Touriga Nacional (Douro), Aglianico, Fiano and Nero d'Avola (southern Italy). Learn them with their region and their main style.

Frequently asked questions

How many grape varieties are there in WSET Level 3?

There is no single fixed list to memorise: the course covers the main international varieties in depth and many local grapes region by region. The key is to know their style and how place and winemaking change it.

What is the best way to memorise grapes for WSET 3?

Short daily practice with mixed questions (grape ↔ region ↔ style) plus flashcards works better than reading lists. The Wine Goat repeats the grapes you get wrong until you know them.

Do I need to know grape varieties for the tasting?

You are not asked to name the grape in the tasting exam, but knowing grape profiles helps you describe structure and reach the right quality conclusions.
