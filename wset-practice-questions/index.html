WSET practice questions online, with answers and explanations

In short

Practice questions are the fastest way to turn what you read into knowledge you can retrieve in the exam. Answer a short set after every lesson, read why each wrong option is wrong, and keep your mistakes for review. Below you will find 12 sample questions with answers across Wine Levels 1, 2 and 3 and spirits.

Updated 2026-11-18 · The Wine Goat

The WSET exams at Levels 1 and 2 are entirely multiple-choice, and Wine Level 3 starts with 50 multiple-choice questions before the written answers. Being good at this format is a skill in itself: it rewards precise knowledge, careful reading and a calm pace.

This page explains how to use practice questions well, shares a simple technique for answering multiple-choice questions, and gives you 12 sample questions with explanations so you can test yourself right now.

Why do practice questions work?

Answering a question forces your memory to retrieve information, and every successful retrieval makes the next one easier. That is why testing yourself beats rereading, even when you get answers wrong.

Reading a chapter twice feels like progress because the text looks familiar. But familiarity is not recall: in the exam you have to produce the answer from memory, among three plausible alternatives. Practice questions train exactly that. They also show you, quickly and honestly, which topics you really know and which ones you only recognise.

Good questions come with explanations. A short note on why the right answer is right consolidates the fact; a deeper look at why each other option is wrong teaches you the distinctions the exam is built on, such as two regions that grow the same grape or two methods that sound alike.

How to use practice questions in your revision

Use questions at three moments: straight after each lesson, in mixed sets across chapters, and in full mock exams near the end. Short daily sets are more effective than occasional long ones.

MomentWhat to doTypical size
After each lessonQuestions on that chapter only, read every explanation10–15 questions
Daily reviewMixed questions from chapters you have already studied, plus your past mistakes15–25 questions
Final weeksFull timed mock exams in the real format30 or 50 questions
  • Write down or save every question you get wrong and revisit it a few days later.
  • If you guessed correctly, treat it as a mistake: read the explanation anyway.
  • Mix chapters as soon as you can, because the real exam does not tell you the topic in advance.
  • Track your score per chapter rather than your overall average.

Multiple-choice technique for WSET exams

Read the whole question, predict the answer before looking at the options, eliminate the clearly wrong ones and only then choose. Never leave a question blank: there is no penalty for a wrong answer at Levels 1 and 2.

Read the stem carefully

Many questions hinge on one word: "main", "typically", "minimum", "not", "most likely". Underline it mentally before you read the options. A question asking which grape is not permitted is the opposite of one asking which grape is typical, and in a hurry they look the same.

Predict, then compare

Try to answer before reading the four options. If your prediction is among them, it is very probably correct. If not, reread the question: you may have misread it.

Eliminate distractors

Wrong options are rarely absurd. They are often a real grape from the wrong region, a real technique with the wrong effect, or a correct fact that does not answer the question. Removing two options usually leaves a 50-50 choice you can reason through.

Manage your time

You have 90 seconds per question at Level 1 and 72 seconds at Level 2. Answer the easy ones quickly, flag the difficult ones and come back to them at the end with a fresh eye.

Change an answer only if you find a concrete reason, such as a word you misread. Second-guessing without new information more often turns a right answer into a wrong one.

Sample questions: Wine Level 1

Level 1 questions test the main grapes, styles, service and food pairing. They are short and direct; precision is what matters.

1 Which of these grape varieties is used to make white wine?

Show the answer

Answer: Riesling

Why: Riesling is a white grape variety, one of the eight principal grapes. Merlot, Syrah and Pinot Noir are all black grapes used for red (and sometimes rosé) wines.

2 How does salt in food usually affect the taste of a wine?

Show the answer

Answer: It makes the wine taste less bitter and less acidic

Why: Salt, like acidity in food, makes a wine taste softer: less bitter and less acidic. Sweetness and umami in food have the opposite effect, making wine taste more bitter and acidic.

3 Which of these wines should be served well chilled?

Show the answer

Answer: A sparkling wine

Why: Sparkling and sweet wines are served well chilled. Full- and medium-bodied reds are served at room temperature, while light-bodied reds can be lightly chilled.

Sample questions: Wine Level 2

Level 2 questions connect grapes, regions, winemaking and labels. Expect plausible distractors from the same country or technique family.

1 Sancerre, in the Loire Valley, is mainly made from which grape variety?

Show the answer

Answer: Sauvignon Blanc

Why: Sancerre is a classic Loire Valley wine made from Sauvignon Blanc. Chenin Blanc is also a Loire grape, which makes it a typical distractor, but it is associated with other Loire appellations such as Vouvray.

2 What is the main effect of malolactic conversion on a wine?

Show the answer

Answer: It softens the acidity and can add buttery flavours

Why: Malolactic conversion turns sharp malic acid into softer lactic acid, lowering the perceived acidity and often adding buttery notes. It does not change the alcohol level, add tannin or create sweetness.

3 Which method is typically used to make Prosecco?

Show the answer

Answer: The tank method

Why: Prosecco is typically made by the tank method, with the second fermentation in a sealed tank, which preserves fresh, fruity aromas. The traditional method, with the second fermentation in the bottle, is used for Champagne.

Sample questions: Wine Level 3

Level 3 multiple-choice questions focus on causes and effects. Knowing the name is not enough: you must know why it matters.

1 What is the main effect of a large diurnal range on ripening grapes?

Show the answer

Answer: Cool nights help the grapes retain acidity and aromas

Why: A large difference between day and night temperatures lets grapes ripen in warm days while cool nights slow the loss of acidity and help preserve aromatic compounds. It is not linked to hail, and it does not stop sugar or tannin ripening.

2 What does extended lees contact typically add to a still white wine?

Show the answer

Answer: Body, texture and yeasty or bready notes

Why: Ageing on the lees adds body and a creamy texture, and can bring yeasty, bready flavours. Lees contact does not add tannin or colour, does not raise acidity and does not make a wine sweet.

3 How is the sweetness of Port achieved?

Show the answer

Answer: By adding grape spirit during fermentation, which stops it

Why: Port is fortified during fermentation: the grape spirit raises the alcohol level and stops the yeast, leaving natural grape sugar in the wine. This is why Port is both sweet and high in alcohol.

Sample questions: Spirits

Spirits questions often test raw materials, legal rules and label terms. Numbers such as minimum percentages and ageing periods are easy marks once memorised.

1 What is a legal requirement for Bourbon?

Show the answer

Answer: It must be made from a mash of at least 51% corn

Why: Bourbon must be made from at least 51% corn and aged in new charred oak barrels. It can be made anywhere in the United States, not only in Kentucky; 100% malted barley is the rule for single malt whisky.

2 A Tequila labelled "joven" or "oro" is usually:

Show the answer

Answer: Unaged spirit, often with colour added

Why: Joven or oro (gold) Tequila is usually unaged, with colour added; the golden hue does not mean oak ageing. Reposado and añejo are the terms for Tequilas that have spent time in oak.

3 What is the minimum ageing for a Cognac labelled VSOP?

Show the answer

Answer: At least 4 years

Why: VSOP Cognac must be aged for at least four years in oak. VS requires at least two years and XO at least ten, so these terms make classic distractors for each other.

How to learn from a wrong answer

A wrong answer is the most useful thing a practice session can give you, provided you deal with it in three steps: understand it, connect it, and see it again later.

  • Understand: read the explanation and say in one sentence why the correct answer is right.
  • Connect: link the fact to something you already know, such as the climate of the region or the effect of the technique, so it is not an isolated detail.
  • Compare: if you confused two similar things, write the difference between them side by side.
  • Repeat: answer the same question again a few days later, and once more the week before the exam.

Spaced repetition does this automatically in The Wine Goat: the questions and flashcards you miss come back sooner, and the ones you know come back less often, so your time goes where it is needed.

Common mistakes when practising

Practice questions only help if you use them to learn, not just to score.

  • Doing hundreds of questions without reading the explanations.
  • Repeating the same set until you remember the answers instead of the reasons.
  • Practising only your favourite chapters and avoiding the hard ones.
  • Never timing yourself before the real exam.
  • Ignoring lucky guesses, which hide real gaps.

Anatomy of a WSET-style question

Every multiple-choice question has three parts: a stem that asks the question, one key that answers it, and three distractors built from real facts used in the wrong place. Seeing the structure helps you read questions the way they were written.

Take a typical Level 2 item: "Which grape variety is mainly used for red Rioja?" The key is Tempranillo. The distractors might be Garnacha, which is genuinely grown in Rioja but is not the main grape; Nebbiolo, a famous black grape from the wrong country; and Malbec, a grape strongly associated with another region. Each wrong option tests a different confusion: a real partner grape, a real grape from another place, and a well-known name that sounds plausible. The word "mainly" is what turns Garnacha from tempting into wrong.

Four kinds of distractor, and how to beat them

Most wrong options fall into a few families. Recognising the family tells you what knowledge the question is really checking.

Distractor typeExampleWhat beats it
Right fact, wrong placeSauvignon Blanc offered for a red Burgundy questionLearning regions and grapes as pairs, in both directions
Real but secondaryGarnacha for "mainly" in RiojaReading qualifiers such as main, typically, minimum
Right process, wrong effectMalolactic conversion "increasing acidity"Learning each process with its effect, not just its name
True statement, wrong questionA correct fact about Port in a question about SherryChecking that the option answers this stem, not just that it is true

Eight more sample questions

These questions mix levels and include more demanding distractors. Answer each one before opening the explanation, and notice which kind of distractor tempted you.

1 Chianti Classico is mainly made from which grape variety?

Show the answer

Answer: Sangiovese

Why: Chianti, in Tuscany, is based on Sangiovese. Nebbiolo and Barbera are Piedmont grapes, and Montepulciano is a grape grown mainly in central and southern Italy, which makes it a classic "sounds like the region" trap.

2 What does the term "Brut" indicate on a sparkling wine label?

Show the answer

Answer: A dry style

Why: Brut is a sweetness term and indicates a dry style. It says nothing about the production method or the ageing period, which are what the other options describe.

3 Why are vineyards in cool regions of the Northern Hemisphere often planted on south-facing slopes?

Show the answer

Answer: To catch more sunlight and warmth

Why: South-facing slopes receive more direct sunlight in the Northern Hemisphere, helping grapes ripen in marginal climates. Better ripening increases sugar rather than reducing it.

4 Which winemaking technique is most associated with the fruity red wines of Beaujolais?

Show the answer

Answer: Carbonic maceration

Why: Whole bunches fermenting in a carbon dioxide-rich environment give the soft tannins and bright red-fruit, sometimes banana-like, aromas typical of many Beaujolais wines. Lees stirring is mainly a white winemaking technique.

5 Which type of still typically produces a lighter, more neutral spirit?

Show the answer

Answer: A column still

Why: A column still allows continuous distillation to high strength, removing more flavour compounds and giving a lighter, more neutral spirit. Pot stills generally give fuller, more flavourful spirits.

6 What is Tequila made from?

Show the answer

Answer: Blue agave

Why: Tequila is made from blue agave. Sugar cane is the raw material of rum, barley of many whiskies and grapes of brandy, each a real raw material for the wrong spirit.

7 What is the minimum ageing for Scotch whisky?

Show the answer

Answer: Three years in oak in Scotland

Why: Scotch whisky must be matured in oak casks in Scotland for at least three years. The other options borrow numbers and rules that belong to other categories.

8 What is the main difference between a Fino and an Oloroso Sherry?

Show the answer

Answer: Fino ages under a layer of flor, Oloroso ages oxidatively

Why: Fino matures under flor, a layer of yeast that protects it from oxygen and keeps it pale and fresh; Oloroso matures without flor, developing a darker colour and nutty flavours. Both are fortified and both are made dry.

Mini case: stuck at 60%

A Level 2 candidate has scored around 60% in mixed sets for two weeks. More questions alone have not helped. The fix starts with the data: sorting the last 100 wrong answers shows that a third are confusions between regions in the same country and a quarter are misread stems. The candidate builds ten contrast cards for the confused pairs, reads every stem twice for qualifiers, and practises in smaller, slower sets for a week. Mixed scores climb towards 70%, because the problem was never a lack of effort but a few repeated patterns.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find WSET practice questions with answers?

The Wine Goat offers multiple-choice practice questions with answers and explanations for Wine Levels 1, 2 and 3 and Spirits Levels 1 and 2. Wine Level 1 and Spirits Level 1 are free. Each question explains why the right answer is right and why the other options are wrong, and your mistakes come back for review.

Are these the real WSET exam questions?

No. Our questions are written by our own team to follow the published syllabuses and to reproduce the style and difficulty of the exam. They are not past papers. Their purpose is to help you practise retrieving the knowledge the exam tests, not to memorise specific questions.

How many practice questions should I do per day?

Quality matters more than quantity. Around 10 to 15 questions after each lesson, plus a mixed set of 15 to 25 questions on topics you have already studied, is a good daily rhythm. What makes the difference is reading every explanation and revisiting your mistakes a few days later.

Is there negative marking in WSET multiple-choice exams?

At Levels 1 and 2, each correct answer earns one mark and a wrong answer simply earns nothing, so there is no reason to leave any question blank. If you are unsure, eliminate the options you know are wrong and make your best choice from what remains.

Do practice questions help with the Level 3 written answers?

Multiple-choice questions build the knowledge you need, but written answers are a separate skill. At Wine Level 3, also practise open questions: The Wine Goat includes 101 of them with model answers and marking points, so you can check whether each idea is explained clearly enough to earn its mark.

Are WSET multiple-choice questions designed to be tricky?

They are designed to be precise rather than tricky. Wrong options are usually real facts placed in the wrong context, so a vague memory is not enough. Read the full stem, look for qualifiers such as "main" or "minimum", predict the answer before reading the options and eliminate what does not fit. Candidates who learn grapes, regions and processes as linked pairs find most questions straightforward.

Should I start practice questions before I finish the course?

Yes. Start with questions on each chapter as soon as you have studied it, even in the first week. Early testing shows what did not stick while the lesson is still fresh, and it strengthens memory more than rereading. Leave full mixed mock exams for later, when you have covered most of the syllabus, but do not wait until the end to start testing yourself.

What practice score shows I am ready for the exam?

Look for consistent scores in mixed sets and mocks comfortably above the threshold you are aiming for. At Level 2, many candidates use about 70% in mixed practice as a sign of a safe pass, and around 85% or more for a realistic distinction attempt. Check chapter scores too: one very weak chapter can cost several marks on the day.

Can I practise WSET questions in my own language?

In The Wine Goat you can practise in English, Italian, French or Spanish, with separate versions for Spain and Argentina. A useful approach is to learn difficult concepts in the language you think in and then answer questions in the language of your exam, so the exact terminology becomes familiar before the day.
