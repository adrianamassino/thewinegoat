WSET Spirits Practice Questions (with Answers and Explanations)

In short

Here are 20 multiple-choice questions in the style of the WSET Spirits exams: 8 for Level 1, 8 for Level 2 and 4 harder Level 3-style questions. Every question shows the right answer and explains why the other options are wrong, so you learn from mistakes, not just scores.

Updated 2026-11-18 · The Wine Goat

Practice questions are the fastest way to find out what you really know before a spirits exam. Reading a chapter makes it feel familiar. A good multiple-choice question then shows whether you can tell VSOP from XO, low wines from hearts, or a Collins from a sour when four plausible answers sit side by side.

These questions are original, written to mirror the format and level of each paper. They are not past papers, which are never released. Try each question before reading the explanation, and keep a note of the topics you miss: that list is your revision plan.

How the WSET Spirits exams use multiple-choice questions

All three levels use multiple-choice questions with one correct answer and no marks lost for wrong answers, so you should never leave a question blank. What changes is the depth, the time you have and the pass mark.

LevelMultiple-choice partTimePass markOther parts
Level 130 questions45 minutes70%None
Level 250 questions60 minutes55% (merit 70%, distinction 85%)None
Level 350 questions2 h 10 shared with the short answers55% for this part10 short written answers and a blind tasting of 2 spirits

Level 1 checks core facts: production stages, main categories, tasting and mixing basics. Level 2 adds still parts, label terms, legal minimums and cocktail families, with its 50 questions split 14 / 23 / 7 / 6 across production, main spirits, flavoured spirits and aromatised wines, and service. Level 3 questions often describe a situation and ask you to apply a principle.

Level 1 practice questions

Production, Cognac, vodka, whiskey, gin, Tequila and cocktail balance, weighted like the real paper towards the spirit categories.

1 What is the main purpose of distillation in spirits production?

Show the answer

Answer: To concentrate the alcohol from a fermented liquid

Why: Fermentation creates the alcohol; distillation boils the fermented liquid and condenses the vapours, which are richer in ethanol than the liquid they came from. Sugar, colour and starch conversion all belong to other stages.

2 A fermented liquid that is ready for the still is typically around which strength?

Show the answer

Answer: 8-10% abv

Why: Fermented liquids are usually around 8-10% abv. 25-30% is the low wines, 40% the usual bottling strength and about 75% a typical new pot still spirit.

3 On a Cognac label, what is the minimum age of the youngest spirit in a VSOP?

Show the answer

Answer: 4 years

Why: Age terms on Cognac guarantee the youngest spirit in the blend: VS at least 2 years, VSOP at least 4 years and XO at least 10 years. Many producers go well beyond these minimums.

4 Why must vodka be made in a column still?

Show the answer

Answer: Only a column still can reach the 95-96% abv needed for a neutral spirit

Why: Vodka starts as a neutral spirit distilled to at least 95% abv (USA) or 96% abv (EU). A pot still, however many times it is used, cannot reach that strength; a tall column with many plates can.

5 Which statement about Bourbon is correct?

Show the answer

Answer: It must be at least 51% corn and aged in new charred oak barrels

Why: Bourbon can be made anywhere in the USA, though Kentucky dominates. It needs at least 51% corn, is usually column-distilled to a low strength and must go into new charred oak.

6 What does the term 'London Dry Gin' tell you?

Show the answer

Answer: The flavours were added by re-distillation, and the gin can be made anywhere

Why: London Dry is a production method, not a place of origin. It tells you the botanical flavours came from re-distilling the spirit with them. A gin labelled this way can be produced in any country.

7 How long is a Reposado Tequila aged in oak?

Show the answer

Answer: Between 2 and 12 months

Why: Reposado means 'rested': 2 to 12 months in oak, which softens the spirit without hiding the agave. Blanco is unaged, Añejo has at least 12 months and Extra Añejo at least three years.

8 In a balanced cocktail, which of these is part of the 'weak' element?

Show the answer

Answer: Water from melting ice

Why: The 'weak' side covers anything that lowers the strength: water from ice during stirring or shaking, plus juices, sodas, tonic and dairy. The base spirit is the strong element; liqueurs bring sweetness and bitters bring bitterness.

Level 2 practice questions

Level 2 expects you to know how things work, not only what they are called. Watch for answers that are nearly right: a number from the wrong stage, a term from the wrong country or a cocktail from the neighbouring family.

1 In double pot still distillation, what is the typical strength of the low wines?

Show the answer

Answer: 25-30% abv

Why: The first distillation mainly removes water. The distiller keeps collecting until almost all the alcohol is out of the pot, so the low wines end up at only about 25-30% abv. The second distillation then lifts the hearts to about 70-75%.

2 In a column still, what is the job of the downcomer?

Show the answer

Answer: To carry overflowing liquid down to the plate below

Why: Vapour rises through the bubble caps, while liquid that builds up on a plate overflows through the downcomer to the plate below. This counter-flow lets the column run continuously.

3 Which grape variety gives many Armagnacs a typical prune aroma?

Show the answer

Answer: Baco

Why: Ugni Blanc is the most planted variety in both Cognac and Armagnac, but Armagnac makes more use of other varieties. Baco is linked to prune notes, while Folle Blanche gives floral, fruity spirits, which makes it well suited to unaged Blanche Armagnac.

4 What does 'Straight' mean on an American whiskey label?

Show the answer

Answer: At least two years in new oak with no added colour or flavour

Why: Straight Bourbon or Straight Rye has been aged for at least two years in new oak, and nothing has been added for colour or flavour. If the whiskey is under four years old, an age statement must also appear on the label.

5 Where does the smoky aroma typical of many Mezcals come from?

Show the answer

Answer: Roasting the piñas in stone-lined pits

Why: Traditional Mezcal producers cook the agave hearts in pits heated by stones from a wood fire, then cover them with earth. This roasting adds smoke. Tequila agave is usually steamed, which adds no smoky flavour.

6 Which drink belongs to the Collins family?

Show the answer

Answer: Paloma

Why: A Collins is a sour made long with soda over ice. The Paloma follows the same idea with Tequila, lime and grapefruit soda. The Daiquiri and Sidecar are short sours and the Manhattan is spirit-forward.

7 A Scotch whisky labelled 'cask strength' has been...

Show the answer

Answer: bottled without being diluted with water

Why: Most whiskies are reduced with water before bottling; cask strength whiskies skip this step. 'Single cask', 'Sherry cask' and 'natural colour' are separate claims.

8 What is the minimum distillation strength for a neutral spirit in the EU?

Show the answer

Answer: 96% abv

Why: Neutral spirit is defined by law: at least 96% abv in the EU and at least 95% abv in the USA. At 90% the spirit is light rather than neutral, which is typical of light rum marks and Scottish grain whisky.

Level 3-style questions

At Level 3 the multiple-choice part covers more spirits in more detail, and the short answers ask you to explain cause and effect. These four questions practise that way of thinking: why a production choice leads to a particular style.

1 A malt distiller wants a lighter, cleaner new make from the same pot stills. Which change would help most?

Show the answer

Answer: Increase reflux, for example with a slower distillation or a taller still neck

Why: More reflux means more vapour condenses and falls back before reaching the condenser, so only the most volatile fractions get through and the spirit is lighter. Collecting the hearts down to a lower strength lets heavier tail compounds in, worm tubs reduce copper contact, and short fermentations tend to give more cereal-like character: all three push the other way.

2 Which of these is NOT a legal category of Scotch whisky?

Show the answer

Answer: Double Malt

Why: There are five categories: Single Malt, Single Grain, Blended Malt, Blended Grain and Blended Scotch Whisky. 'Double Malt' has no legal meaning. Remember that 'single' refers to one distillery, not one cask.

3 By law, when must the distillation of each Cognac harvest be completed?

Show the answer

Answer: By 31 March of the year after the harvest

Why: Cognac wines are made without added sulfur, so they do not keep long. The rules require distillation to finish by 31 March after the harvest. This is a classic 'state and explain' point.

4 Why do Bourbons usually gain colour and oak flavour faster than Scotch whiskies of the same age?

Show the answer

Answer: New charred barrels and hot warehouse summers speed up extraction

Why: Two things work together. A new charred barrel has the most colour and flavour to give, while Scotch normally uses refilled casks. Kentucky's hot summers also push the spirit in and out of the staves faster than Scotland's cooler climate. In an 'explain' answer, give both the cause and the effect.

How to score yourself

Score one point per correct answer. For Level 1, 6 out of 8 is about the 70% you need; for Level 2, 5 out of 8 is a pass and 7 is distinction territory. One small set proves little: what matters is the pattern of mistakes.

  • Wrong on numbers (abv, ages, percentages): make a one-page sheet of minimums and test yourself daily.
  • Wrong on processes (stills, maturation): draw the process from memory, then check it.
  • Wrong on labels (Straight, London Dry, 100% agave): group terms by country and write what each one guarantees.
  • Wrong on cocktails: learn the family first, then the drink, using the strong / weak / sweet / sour-bitter pattern.

Common traps in spirits multiple-choice questions

Wrong options are often real facts placed in the wrong context.

TrapExampleHow to avoid it
Right number, wrong stageOffering 25-30% abv as the strength of a new spiritLink each figure to its stage: about 10% after fermentation, 25-30% low wines, about 75% hearts
Place versus methodThinking London Dry Gin must come from LondonAsk whether a term describes where, how or how long
Aroma versus sugarCalling a Bourbon sweet because it smells of vanillaSweetness on the palate only comes from added sugar; all new spirits are dry
Neighbouring familyPutting the Mojito with the short soursCheck whether the drink is topped up with a long, weak mixer
Absolute wordsOptions with 'always', 'only' or 'never'Look for one exception you know; if you find one, the option is wrong

How to build a study routine with practice questions

Short daily sessions of 15 to 20 questions beat one long session the night before, because spaced recall is what fixes facts in long-term memory.

  • While learning: after each topic, answer questions on that topic only and reread every missed explanation.
  • Final weeks: switch to mixed sets, then full timed mock exams.
  • Every day: review yesterday's mistakes before starting new questions.

Reasoning through a hard question, step by step

When you do not know an answer immediately, a structured elimination often finds it. Consider a question asking which spirit is most likely to show grassy, vegetal aromas when unaged, with the options light Cuban-style rum, rhum agricole, molasses-based dark rum and spiced rum.

  • Step 1, name the topic: the question is about the raw material of sugar cane spirits.
  • Step 2, recall the rule: fresh cane juice gives grassy, vegetal notes; molasses gives toffee and brown sugar.
  • Step 3, eliminate: the dark rum is molasses-based; spiced rum is defined by added spices and sweetness; light Cuban-style rum is column-distilled to a higher strength from molasses and is subtle.
  • Step 4, confirm: rhum agricole is made from fresh cane juice in the French Caribbean, so it is the answer.

The same four steps work for most questions: topic, rule, elimination, confirmation. Practising them on easy questions makes them automatic on hard ones.

Write your own questions to learn faster

Writing a question forces you to decide exactly what is true and what is almost true, which is a powerful way to learn. After each topic, write three questions of your own with four options: one correct answer and three plausible wrong ones taken from neighbouring facts. Swap them with a study partner, or answer them yourself a week later.

  • Good distractors are real facts in the wrong place: the VSOP age offered for XO, a Scotch term offered for Bourbon.
  • Avoid trick wording; the real exams test knowledge, not reading traps.
  • Add a one-line explanation, as in the questions above. If you cannot write it, you have found a gap in your knowledge.

Practice prompts for Level 3 short answers

Multiple choice is only part of Level 3. These prompts mirror the style of the written questions; answer each one in bullet sentences that link a production choice to its effect, then check your notes.

  • Explain how a long fermentation can increase the fruitiness of a sugar cane spirit. (5 marks)
  • Describe the legal requirements that apply to all Scotch whisky. (5 marks)
  • Explain how the design and operation of a pot still can produce a heavier or a lighter spirit. (10 marks)
  • Explain why a Cognac from the same eaux-de-vie can taste very different after ageing in new and in old casks. (10 marks)
  • Explain how a gin producer's choices, from base spirit to botanicals and method of redistillation, shape the style of the gin. (20 marks)

Frequently asked questions

Are there official WSET Spirits past papers?

No. Past papers are not released for any spirits level, and the specifications only include a few sample questions. The best substitute is a large bank of original questions written to the same format and level, with explanations, so that each mistake teaches you something. Use them alongside the course materials and your class notes, not instead of them.

How many questions are in the WSET Level 1 Spirits exam?

The Level 1 Award in Spirits exam has 30 multiple-choice questions to be answered in 45 minutes. You need 70%, which is 21 correct answers, to pass, and there are no merit or distinction grades at this level. Most questions are about the main spirit categories, with fewer on production, tasting and mixing.

What is the pass mark for WSET Level 2 Spirits?

You need 55% to pass, which means 28 of the 50 questions. A score of 70% or more earns a pass with merit and 85% or more a pass with distinction. The paper lasts one hour and wrong answers are not penalised, so make sure you answer every question.

How many practice questions should I do before the exam?

There is no magic number, but most well-prepared candidates answer several hundred questions across the syllabus and sit at least two or three full timed mock exams. Quality matters more than volume: read every explanation, including for questions you got right by guessing, and come back to your weak topics a few days later.

Are the Level 3 Spirits exam questions harder?

Yes, in two ways. The multiple-choice part covers many more spirits in much greater detail, and the theory exam adds ten short written answers that ask you to state, describe or explain production choices and their effects. You must reach 55% in each part of the exam, including the blind tasting, to pass.

What topics come up most often in spirits exams?

At every level the biggest share of marks goes to the spirit categories: what they are made from, how they are made, what the label terms mean and how they taste. Production principles come next, especially pot versus column stills and maturation. Cocktail families and balance also appear at Levels 1 and 2.

Is it worth guessing in the WSET Spirits multiple-choice exam?

Always. Marks are not deducted for wrong answers at any level, so a blank answer is a guaranteed zero while a guess gives you at least a 25% chance. Improve your odds by eliminating options that you know are false, especially those built on absolute words like 'always' or 'only'.

How accurate are online WSET spirits mock exams?

Independent mock exams cannot reproduce the real papers, which are not published, but good ones follow the same format, timing and syllabus weighting. Use them to measure progress and find weak areas rather than to predict an exact score. If you consistently score well above the pass mark on mixed, timed sets, you are usually well prepared for the real exam.

What score on practice questions means I am ready?

Aim for a comfortable margin above your target. For Level 1, scoring 85% or more on timed sets of 30 questions gives a safe cushion above the 70% pass mark. For Level 2, around 75% suggests a pass is secure, and around 90% is the zone where distinction becomes realistic, since exam nerves usually take away a few points.

Should I memorise practice questions and their answers?

No. The real exam uses different questions, so memorising answers gives a false sense of security. Focus on the explanation behind each answer: why the right option is right and why each wrong option is wrong. If you can explain a question to someone else without looking at the answer, you have learned the underlying fact.

Are there practice questions for the WSET Level 3 Spirits tasting?

The tasting exam is not multiple choice, so practice means writing full tasting notes on spirits served blind. Use pairs from the examinable list, allow 15 minutes per sample, and check that your note covers appearance, nose, palate, a reasoned quality level and an identification. Practice multiple-choice questions remain useful for the theory part.
